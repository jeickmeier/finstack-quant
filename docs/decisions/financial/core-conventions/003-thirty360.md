## Choice, scope and rationale

All variants compute `N = 360*(Y2-Y1) + 30*(M2-M1) + D2' - D1'`, with year fraction `N/360`. Contract selection must distinguish fixed US 30U/360 EOM, ISDA bond basis, European, Italian and 30E/360 ISDA. The source's SIA/PSA and other alias descriptions require correction; their wording does not establish equivalence between distinct formulas.

## Accepted decision: synthetic day-count conventions

Accepted on **2026-11-07** after whole-record review. This complete package
selects the five formulas, their public selection and interval semantics
together. Runtime reconciliation and independent qualification remain open;
the observed source behavior remains separate below.

1. **Select five distinct financial conventions explicitly.** Preserve
   `Thirty360` / `30_360` for the existing fixed US EOM algorithm,
   `ThirtyE360` / `30e_360` for European, `Thirty360It` / `30_360_it` for
   Italian, and `ThirtyE360Isda` / `30e_360_isda` for the ISDA European
   termination-sensitive variant. Add the distinct `Thirty360Isda` /
   `30_360_isda` selection for the already implemented ISDA bond-basis
   formula. The new name must not select `ThirtyE360Isda`. Keep canonical
   case/outer-whitespace parsing normalization without ambiguous aliases.
   Product records own contractual selection; do not infer it from currency,
   a generic bond label or another clock. Do not automatically roll the
   supplied dates to business days.

2. **Adopt these exact synthetic-day adjustments.** For a strictly forward
   interval use the original calendar years/months and adjusted day numbers
   in `N = 360*(Y2-Y1) + 30*(M2-M1) + D2' - D1'`, then return `N/360`.

   | Financial convention | Start adjustment | End adjustment |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | US fixed 30U/360 EOM | Day 31 or February month-end becomes 30. | Day 31 becomes 30 when adjusted start is 30; February month-end becomes 30 when original start is also February month-end. |
   | ISDA bond basis | Day 31 becomes 30. | Day 31 becomes 30 when adjusted start is 30; no February month-end rule. |
   | European 30E/360 | Day 31 becomes 30. | Independently, day 31 becomes 30; no February rule. |
   | Italian | Day 31 or any February day greater than 27 becomes 30. | Apply the same rule independently. |
   | 30E/360 ISDA | Day 31 or February month-end becomes 30. | Day 31 always becomes 30; February month-end becomes 30 unless it is the contractual termination date. |

   The three ISDA-defined formulas follow
   [2006 Definitions sections 4.16(f), (g), (h)](https://www.sc.com/en/uploads/sites/66/content/docs/2006-ISDA-Definitions.pdf).
   The US and Italian choices match the named algorithms in the
   [QuantLib implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lballabio/QuantLib/master/ql/time/daycounters/thirty360.cpp);
   this is source comparison, not regenerated vendor qualification.

3. **Describe the US variant and vendor names precisely.** The selected
   US formula is fixed `30U/360 EOM`, including its paired February-end
   adjustment. Do not call PSA an interchangeable synonym: the
   [official Strata descriptions](https://strata.opengamma.io/day_counts/#30u360-eom)
   distinguish it from `30/360 PSA`, which lacks that paired-end adjustment.
   Do not add a schedule-dependent EOM switch to this fixed algorithm;
   the no-February ISDA bond-basis choice is a separate explicit selection.
   Vendor enum names are not portable identities: QuantLib `BondBasis` /
   `ISMA` implements the ISDA bond-basis formula, while its `ISDA` means
   the local `ThirtyE360Isda`. Reconcile prose, external-code mappings and
   fixtures against the actual selected definition and reference edition,
   rather than mapping an ambiguous label to a different formula.

4. **Require explicit termination status for 30E/360 ISDA.** Change the
   existing context's `end_is_termination_date` to an optional Boolean,
   with omission meaning unspecified rather than false. Selecting
   `ThirtyE360Isda` requires an explicit true or false, including for
   equal-date queries. It describes the chronological later accrual
   endpoint, regardless of signed argument order. True means that endpoint
   is the actual contractual termination date; false explicitly selects an
   ordinary endpoint. The caller supplies this contractual fact; do not
   infer it from February, a delayed payment date or the last available
   observation. The exception preserves February 28/29 only; a terminal
   day 31 still becomes 30. Other conventions do not require this field,
   and valid irrelevant context remains allowed. Use the existing runtime
   and serializable context, not a competing maturity/context framework.

5. **Use one public interval boundary.** Make `DayCount` the public
   selector for all five choices through `year_fraction` and
   `signed_year_fraction`. Make the raw formula helpers implementation
   details; remove the public `days_30_360`, `days_30e_360_isda` and redundant
   `Thirty360Convention` exports and host wrappers. Do not retain unchecked
   public paths, compatibility wrappers or a second selectable family.
   Raw formula values on equal or reversed arguments do not define public
   interval semantics. Exact integer numerators remain internal to the
   financial calculation.

6. **Preserve context during signed reversal and validate before zero.**
   Apply [001's accepted structural context boundary](001-actual-day-counts.md#accepted-decision-actual-day-conventions)
   consistently to runtime, restored and host inputs, then check the selected
   convention's required termination status. Valid equal dates return zero,
   even where an unguarded terminal-February formula would return a negative
   numerator. The unsigned API rejects reversed dates. The signed API
   calculates the chronological forward span with the same convention and
   later-endpoint termination status, then negates it. Remove the current
   clearing of that status. Do not obtain signed results by directly
   swapping arguments in an unguarded synthetic formula.

7. **Preserve synthetic-day meaning and numerical limits.** Count `N`
   exactly with checked integer arithmetic over the supported `Date` domain
   before conversion and division by 360. Forward results must be finite
   and nonnegative; distinct dates may legitimately give zero. Synthetic
   counts can exceed elapsed actual days, so Bus252's `count <= actual days`
   check does not apply here. Do not impose strict positivity, an actual-day
   interpretation, arbitrary horizon limits or blanket interval additivity.
   Consumers requiring positive time retain their own
   [clock-eligibility boundary](../conventions/0045-financial-clocks-and-date-boundaries.md#accepted-amendment-volatility-clocks-and-fixing-eligibility).
   Missing/invalid context, arithmetic overflow and nonfinite results are
   explicit errors; no day floor, clipping, fabricated zero for failures or
   substitution of another convention is permitted.

8. **Reconcile the canonical contract across hosts and evidence.** The
   new bond-basis variant, termination-field presence, removal of raw exports,
   errors, wire/schema forms, Python/WASM declarations, parity ledger and
   examples must change together in the implementation slice. Keep the
   existing kernel/context ownership. Correct the stored leap-year
   February-28 description and unsupported certification/alias claims;
   regenerate independent reference cases with exact vendor variants and
   termination inputs recorded. Acceptance records methodology;
   implementation and independent qualification remain separately tracked
   in the [owning follow-ups](../FOLLOW_UPS.md#core-conventions).

## Boundary examples for the accepted package

These are independently calculated synthetic-day examples, not executed
production tests or regenerated vendor output. Each assumes valid context.

| Context and dates | Required result |
| --- | --- |
| `2025-02-28 → 2025-03-31` | US EOM `30/360`; ISDA bond basis `33/360`; European `32/360`; Italian `30/360`; 30E/360 ISDA `30/360`. |
| `2024-02-29 → 2025-02-28` | US EOM `360/360`; a distinct PSA formula would give `358/360`. |
| 30E/360 ISDA, `2011-08-31 → 2012-02-29` | Ordinary end `180/360`; explicit termination end `179/360`. |
| Same final interval, signed reverse with the same termination fact | `-179/360`; clearing the fact would incorrectly give `-180/360`. |
| 30E/360 ISDA, `2025-06-30 → 2025-07-31`, termination true | `30/360`; the termination exception does not preserve July 31. |
| European `2025-01-30 → 2025-01-31`; Italian `2024-02-28 → 2024-02-29` | Zero, despite distinct calendar dates. |
| 30E/360 ISDA, ordinary `2025-02-27 → 2025-02-28` | `3/360` despite one elapsed actual day. |
| US EOM or ISDA bond basis, `2025-01-29 → 2025-01-31 → 2025-02-01` | Separate synthetic counts `2 + 1` differ from unsplit count `2`. |
| Equal February-end dates, 30E/360 ISDA with explicit termination true | Public result zero; an unguarded raw numerator would be `-2` on `2025-02-28`. |
| 30E/360 ISDA with unspecified termination status, including equal dates | Input error. |

## Observed algorithm and actual selection

| Variant | Start day adjustment | End day adjustment | Reachability |
|---|---|---|---|
| US fixed 30U/360 EOM (source calls it SIA/PSA) | 31 or February month-end becomes 30 | 31 becomes 30 if adjusted start is 30; February month-end becomes 30 only when start is also February month-end | `DayCount::Thirty360` and `days_30_360(UsSia)` |
| ISDA bond basis | 31 becomes 30 | 31 becomes 30 if adjusted start is 30 | Public `days_30_360(Isda)` only; no `DayCount` variant |
| European | 31 becomes 30 | 31 becomes 30 | `ThirtyE360` or raw helper |
| Italian | 31 or February day >27 becomes 30 | Same independent rule | `Thirty360It` or raw helper |
| 30E/360 ISDA | Any month-end becomes 30 | 31 becomes 30; February month-end becomes 30 unless end is termination | `ThirtyE360Isda`, flag in context, or `days_30e_360_isda` |

## Conventions, controls and failure

The termination flag defaults false and applies only to the 30E/360 ISDA final-period exception. Ordinary `year_fraction` rejects reversed ranges and short-circuits equal dates to zero. `signed_year_fraction` reverses the dates, negates and deliberately clears termination status. Raw helpers do no date-order or equality validation; their adjusted-day formulas need not be antisymmetric or produce zero on every identical date. For example US SIA on identical February month-end dates adjusts both to 30; 30E/360 ISDA with termination true can retain end day while adjusting start, so callers needing interval semantics should use `DayCount`.

## Alternatives and tradeoffs

The observed ISDA bond-basis choice is reachable only through raw helpers; the accepted contract adds its distinct high-level selection and removes the redundant public raw family. Treating `Thirty360` as a generic alias for ISDA would change February accrual. Explicit termination status remains caller-owned and must be propagated from the contractual schedule, including through signed reversal.
