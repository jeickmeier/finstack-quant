# STATEMENTS-CAPITAL-005: Residual coupon rebuilding after dated balance changes

**Decision status:** Accepted for the full residual coupon rebuilding contract (2026-11-15).
**Implementation status:** Reconciled (2026-11-17). Statements infers no rate and rewrites no coupon row. The waterfall reports the principal it changed outside the contractual schedule in exact money (prepayments paid, less the coupon a PIK toggle capitalized), each change is appended to the instrument's posting ledger effective the day after its period close, and the instrument's owner recompiles the schedule from its own terms and the whole ledger (`TermLoan::cashflow_schedule_with_postings`, `RevolvingCredit::cashflow_schedule_with_postings`): affected coupons accrue on the dated principal under the contract's rate, day count and cash/PIK split, balance-based fees follow the changed balance, later amortization and the maturity or terminal repayment are sized on what remains, and the commitment and terms survive a zero balance. The close day still earns on the old principal. Interest earned before a change inside a coupon is kept whole in accrued interest: a term loan emits a coupon row per balance segment and a revolver closes an interest row at each posting. Dated arrears and advance reserves stay with their own claims in the ledger. The debt ledger and the rebuilt schedules are staged together and committed only when every schedule rebuilds and carries the ledger's closing balance into the next period (to half of the currency's minor unit, the bound the opening check and the full-paydown floor also use); otherwise the period fails and nothing changes. An instrument that cannot rebuild is an error: a PIK toggle may target only a term loan or a revolver (checked when the model is validated), bonds are already excluded from prepayment waterfalls, a revolver rejects a posting on or before its balance anchor (the later of the valuation date and the commitment date), and a coupon toggled to PIK in the period in which a loan matures fails that period, because nothing is left to capitalize into; the contractual PIK record (008) owns that election. The sections below the accepted decision describe the audited implementation.
**Validation status:** Qualified against hand-computed schedules: the record's 10% on 100 then 60 inside one semiannual coupon (2.50 and 1.50, accrued interest at both closes, the 4.00 coupon) and its 75% cash and 25% PIK split (3 and 1, capitalized once on the coupon date); a toggled coupon compounding from the day after its close across four quarters; a swept revolver's interest, usage fee and commitment fee on actual days in a monthly model, with accrued interest at a close between the sweep and the coupon; a fully swept revolver earning again on a contractual redraw; quarter-end amortization with separate settlement dates, an advance reserve and a sweep; unpaid scheduled principal carried as arrears and then a sweep, repaying the face exactly once; a floating coupon rebuilt on its locked fixing, not the forward curve; a coupon toggled in the maturity period; a bond named as a PIK target; and a rejected rebuild leaving the ledger unchanged. The owners' tests cover a posting on ACT/365F inside a coupon with capped later amortization, and postings in another currency, outside the loan's life, before a revolver's anchor and beyond the principal or commitment.
**Audit date:** 2026-11-05. **Source revision:** `7e82fd03ad98346727769e88dd5ac5d84b56e9fc`; [scope and file ledger](README.md).

## Accepted decision for residual coupon rebuilding

1. Rebuild after an authorized dated principal change. A waterfall change posted at period close affects interest earning starting the following day. Preserve settled cash and interest already earned under the product's contractual terms.
2. Rebuild affected original coupon parents through the canonical instrument owner using exact dated principal and the full contractual accrual and rate context. Carry locked observations and growth already earned under the original parent. Error on insufficient inputs or unsupported rebuilding. [Floating coupon replay](../cashflows/08-floating-replay.md#accepted-decision-parent-bound-replay-exact-full-settlement-and-explicit-timing-ownership) · [Schedule lifecycle](../cashflows/12-schedule-lifecycle.md#accepted-decision-validated-complete-schedules-explicit-balances-and-separate-cash-views).
3. Apply the contractual cash/PIK split once to each rebuilt coupon amount. Retain its cash payment date and PIK capitalization date. Preserve the contractual terms through a zero balance so they remain available on an authorized redraw. [Cash and PIK coupon allocation](../cashflows/05-coupon-cash-and-pik.md#accepted-decision-parent-coupon-accrual-exact-splits-and-capitalization).
4. Keep dated arrears and paid advance reserves tied to their original claims. Use canonical term-loan or revolver terms to rebuild affected remaining principal, maturity principal and balance-sensitive fees. Stage the rebuilt schedule and debt ledger together and commit both after validation. [Dated principal claims](04-dated-principal.md#accepted-decision-for-dated-principal-claims) · [Term-loan cashflows](../valuations-other-fixed-income/07-term-loan-cashflows.md#accepted-decision-for-checked-term-loan-cashflow-compilation) · [Revolver cashflows](../valuations-other-fixed-income/10-revolver-cashflows.md#accepted-decision-for-checked-revolver-contractual-cashflows).

A coupon that earns 10% annually on \$100 for an exact quarter year and on \$60 for the next exact quarter year earns \$2.50 and \$1.50. A contractual 75% cash and 25% PIK split produces \$3 of cash interest and \$1 of PIK.

## Audited algorithm and equations

1. Validate nonnegative new closing and schedule currency. Read original economic outstanding at inclusive `from_date` (initial fallback, or zero before issue). Let `δ=new−old`. If `abs(δ)≤1e−9`, return a clone. Otherwise insert a zero-cash PrePayment row at `from_date` with principal delta `δ`; interest feels that change from the following day, which must exist.
2. Reserve positive claims whose economic date is after `from_date`, keyed by `(payment_date,balance_date)`. Reject wrong currency, negative claims or total reserve exceeding new principal by `1e−6`.
3. Sort existing flows by economic date, except cash-interest rows use accrual end when known. On the same date, other principal events precede interest, and terminal Notional redemption follows interest/PIK. Preserve historical rows as anchors.
4. Rebuild Fixed, Stub, FloatReset and PIK. Cash coupons qualify by future payment date; PIK qualifies by future economic date. Use explicit accrual metadata or infer start from the latest earlier same-kind payment date, falling back to issue date; absence errors. Coupons fully earned by the day after the snapshot retain their amounts, even when paid/capitalized later.
5. Use strict calendar lookup and coupon-period/day-count context. For original principal `P` at accrual start, infer signed component rate `r=C/(P·a)` from original cash amount `C` and accrual factor `a`. If `P=0`, use the row's retained rate. Reject nonfinite rates, nonfinite/nonpositive full year fraction or missing anchors.
6. Split accrual at all balance-change dates. For segment fraction `f=DCF(segment)/DCF(full)`, factor `a_s=a·f` and cumulative balance change `Δ`, set `C_s=C·f+Δ·r·a_s`. Preserve payment date, component kind and accrual metadata; retain `r` even when amount becomes zero. Explicit PIK deltas scale with the revised amount (or proportionally by `f` when original amount is zero). This avoids applying an unsplit full coupon rate independently to both cash and PIK shares.
7. Future positive principal payments are capped by remaining balance after outstanding reservations. Future cash Notional redemption takes all available principal; other repayments use their original amount capped to capacity. Already-paid cash is preserved while its future principal delta can be adjusted. Revised future PIK/principal changes update the balance and the change map for later coupons.
8. Reject unmatched reserves remaining above `1e−6`; reconstruct through `CashFlowSchedule::from_flows`, retaining notional/day-count/meta. State rebuild passes both arrears and advance lots and only visits instruments with residual schedules.

## Limitations and alternatives

This is a rate-inference/residual-interest method for supported schedule rows. It is not a fresh contractual model of optional bond calls, prepayment premiums, OID accretion or independently stochastic future resets. FloatReset rows preserve projected component economics from the supplied schedule. Inference assumes canonical constant-principal segments; malformed/custom accrual metadata can fail rather than produce a guessed coupon.

The accepted owner-based rebuild requires contractual inputs for the affected coupon parents and related principal and fee events. The audited approach relies on rate inference from schedule rows.
