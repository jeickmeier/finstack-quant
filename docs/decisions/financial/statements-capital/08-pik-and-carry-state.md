# STATEMENTS-CAPITAL-008: PIK toggles, hysteresis, carried claims and period state

**Decision status:** Accepted for the full PIK election, arrears and carried-state contract (2026-11-15).
**Implementation status:** Reconciled for the modeled forecast exercise (2026-11-17); separately elected observation and notice dates, dated amendments, authorized capitalization of arrears and payoff terms remain open. Authorization is explicit per loan: `pik_toggle.elections` lists each term loan with its `pik_fraction` (the share of each elected coupon that capitalizes, in `(0, 1]`) and its `min_coupon_periods` (owner resolution 3, 2026-11-17); a listed instrument must be a term loan of the capital structure. The threshold must be finite and is in the metric's units; a missing or non-finite metric fails the period. In each forecast period a metric below the threshold requests PIK and a metric at or above it requests the loan's base coupon terms. The request takes effect at the start of the loan's next coupon period after the period close, never on a coupon already running, and only one election exists per coupon period: a later request replaces one that has not started. A PIK election ends only after its minimum number of the loan's coupon periods, counting the first elected one; the count is derived from the election dates, so a period evaluated again reaches the same decision and counts each coupon period once. Actual periods are not exercised: a historical election is recorded on the loan itself. An election is applied by the term loan's own coupon terms (`CouponElection`): the elected share is zero cash and capitalizes once on the coupon date, and later coupons earn on it. The waterfall never moves the current coupon and never transfers arrears: overdue cash interest and fees stay cash claims and are carried separately. A request made when no coupon period is left, including after a full payoff, elects nothing. The election ledger, the debt ledger and the rebuilt schedule are staged and committed together. The sections below the accepted decision describe the audited implementation.
**Validation status:** Qualified against hand-computed schedules: the record's 10 coupon with a 40% election and 3 of overdue cash interest (6 plus 3 of cash, 4 capitalized once, no arrears capitalized); an election starting with the next coupon period and compounding on the coupon dates; minimum durations of zero, one and two coupon periods in a monthly model of a quarterly loan; a period settled three times making one election; a request with no coupon period left after the full payoff; actual periods left unexercised; an unpaid coupon in a period that requests PIK staying overdue cash interest; a non-finite threshold through the typed and wire paths; an election naming a hedge, a bond or an unknown loan; and the owner's test of the 60/40 split and an off-cycle election. Independent agreement examples with notice dates and amendments remain pending. Reconciled to the hand ledger of the [independent qualification](../../../../finstack-quant/statements/src/capital_structure/tests/independent_qualification.rs): a half-coupon election for one interest period, capitalized once on the payment date and earned on afterwards, while unpaid cash interest stays in arrears.

## Accepted decision for contractual PIK elections and carried claims

1. Require explicit PIK authorization for the instrument and coupon. Use actual historical elections and explicitly modeled exercise for forecasts. Apply the contract's observation, notice and election effective dates.
2. Require a finite metric and threshold in matching units. A metric below the threshold requests PIK and a metric at or above it requests base mode, subject to a contractually authorized minimum PIK duration. Count eligible contractual coupon periods, including the first elected period. Reject missing or invalid required inputs.
3. Apply the elected rate and cash/PIK split from canonical coupon terms. Record the PIK component as zero cash and apply its principal increase once on the contractual capitalization date. Carry overdue cash interest and fees separately. Capitalize arrears only through a separately authorized dated action. Require qualified contractual payoff terms for any earned coupon affected by full payoff. [Cash and PIK coupon allocation](../cashflows/05-coupon-cash-and-pik.md#accepted-decision-parent-coupon-accrual-exact-splits-and-capitalization) · [Contractual accrued cash interest](03-funding-and-balances.md#accepted-decision-for-contractual-accrued-cash-interest).
4. Carry state by instrument and election. Apply amendments on their authorized effective dates. Commit election state, the dated debt ledger and the rebuilt schedule atomically. Count each eligible coupon period once across retries. [Dated principal claims](04-dated-principal.md#accepted-decision-for-dated-principal-claims) · [Residual coupon rebuilding](05-residual-coupons.md#accepted-decision-for-residual-coupon-rebuilding).

For example, a \$10 coupon with a 40% PIK election produces \$4 of PIK and \$6 of current cash interest. With \$3 of prior cash-interest arrears, \$9 of cash interest is due and \$4 capitalizes on the contractual date.

## Observed inputs and transitions

`PikToggleSpec` names a liquidity node/formula, threshold in that metric's units, explicit target IDs and a `min_periods_in_pik` count (default zero). The metric must evaluate to a finite number. Initial mode is false with zero active periods. The request is `metric < threshold`, so equality requests cash mode.

For each targeted current instrument: if currently PIK, requested off, and prior consecutive count is below the minimum, remain PIK; otherwise use the request. Increment count each active period and reset to zero when off. Minimum duration measures evaluated reporting periods, not elapsed days or coupons; changing reporting frequency changes its duration.

Prior unpaid cash interest is added to current cash expense before the PIK transfer. If the state's mode is active, move the **entire staged cash-interest expense, including carried arrears**, into PIK and zero cash expense. This precedes all cash caps and ECF deductions. Earned accrued interest is not erased. Fee arrears remain fees and are not toggled. Explicit targets are required; enclosing model validation rejects hedge/option targets, but contractual instrument-level PIK permission/rate step-up is not otherwise modeled.

At close add only the amount moved by the toggle to principal after the principal ledger finishes/dust-floors. Contractual PIK already belongs to dated economic principal and must not be capitalized again. Toggle PIK can create closing principal even after the opening principal has been fully repaid that period. Unpaid interest above `1e−6` is added to accrued and carried to next period; fee arrears remain a separate fee-rung claim; dated principal arrears/advances retain dates.

`advance_period` promotes closing balances into opening using a move, clears closing and current-period principal-flow inputs, and retains mode/counters/arrears/residual schedules. Funding is overwritten per evaluated period. Residual schedules are rebuilt from closing at the inclusive period end as in [05](05-residual-coupons.md).

## Confirmed input gap and other limitations

`PikToggleSpec::validate` checks nonempty targets only; it does not require finite threshold or a nonempty metric string. The metric evaluation path rejects nonfinite metric output, but direct typed `threshold=NaN` is not rejected and `metric < NaN` is false. Positive/negative infinity likewise choose always-on/off requests subject to hysteresis. This is a confirmed static input-contract gap, not a measured downstream loss; wire-format acceptance can differ from direct Rust construction.

Arrears capitalization and full-paydown-period toggle capitalization require explicit contractual permission; the engine does not distinguish coupon entitlement from an unpaid prior claim for this decision. Supplying a different toggle spec midstream does not globally reset all stored modes. These are stateful scenario semantics, not automatic legal rights.
