<DeskContext question="The BBB CLO tranche is offered at 96.5. Which cashflows and collateral losses justify that price?">

Begin with collateral that actually produces cash, then trace its distribution. A tranche rating, coupon or spread cannot explain value without the waterfall, loss allocation, recovery timing and principal rules. The required report includes a manual period reconciliation, native tranche metrics, a default/prepayment grid and an explanation of the quote's downside.

</DeskContext>

<Standard>

### A pool is more than its descriptive averages

The reference contains five positive USD 20 million `PoolAsset` records and USD 100 million of liabilities: AAA 80 million, BBB 15 million and equity 5 million. These priced assets replace the earlier plan's metadata-only representative lines. A `RepLine` can describe a pool, but those descriptions must not be presented as cashflow-producing collateral when the engine consumes `PoolAsset` records.

The initial controlled pool pays fixed 8% interest and repays principal at maturity. Each asset has a maturity, accrual convention, balance, acquisition date and default state. Default and prepayment are competing reductions of performing exposure, while recovery is cash arriving after a lag. Expected loss can be recognized before that recovery cash arrives. Changing recovery rate and changing recovery lag therefore answer different questions.

WAL measures principal timing; it is not duration. WARF is a rating-factor average, not a default probability or a tranche rating. WAS is the weighted spread on performing assets with explicit spreads, not the weighted all-in coupon. We add ratings and floating spread identifiers on a separate copy to demonstrate `clo_warf` and `clo_was` without relabeling the fixed 8% coupon as a 800 bp spread. The common SOFR projection curve is required for that floating copy.

### The waterfall is the central accounting control

For the one-quarter, no-default, no-prepayment, no-fee case, the pool collects USD 100 million principal and USD 2 million interest. AAA receives 80.8 million; BBB receives 15.225 million; equity receives the residual 5.975 million. Every tranche amount is discounted from the same payment date, and their sum equals total available cash. Equity's receipt includes return of its own capital; it is not all profit.

The native waterfall can also route excess interest toward senior principal. Once fees, defaults, coverage tests or reinvestment enter, a coupon-minus-cost shortcut does not reproduce the whole deal. OC compares eligible collateral with the relevant note balance; IC compares available interest with interest obligations. Haircuts and the document's denominator matter. A breached coverage test can redirect cash from juniors to senior repayment, changing timing before principal losses alone explain value.

The first trigger experiment deliberately sets an already-breached 130% threshold to prove the mechanism. A second holds a 124.9% threshold fixed, starts above it, and raises defaults. Comparing enabled and disabled tests at the same CDR separates the additional waterfall effect from the direct effect of losses. The public Python outputs expose resulting PV and WAL; this lesson does not invent an unexposed diversion ledger.

### Deal labels do not install all legal terms

Native CLO, ABS, CMBS and RMBS constructors provide behavioral presets. They still need valid payment calendars and the actual contract's fees and allocation rules. We display their CPR, CDR, recovery and lag defaults, then override terms explicitly. These are teaching assumptions, not underwriting estimates for a named transaction.

CLO management and trustee fees consume cash before residual value. Reinvestment requires both the deal end date and the pool's reinvestment-period configuration; the date alone does not activate recycling. Returning principal to collateral postpones investor repayment and exposes it to subsequent portfolio performance. The example keeps eligibility criteria explicit rather than implying the engine forecasts a manager's security selection.

An ABS step-down switches from sequential toward pro-rata principal only after its date and performance conditions pass. The native trigger is reevaluated each period; it is not necessarily a permanently switched legal state. An excess-spread account must be funded from actual surplus interest and can cover eligible interest shortfalls. A USD 5 million *target* does not mean USD 5 million cash was contributed at closing, and it does not automatically absorb principal writedowns.

PSA is a seasoning curve, with 100% PSA rising toward 6% annual CPR by month 30; monthly mortality is `1-(1-CPR)^(1/12)`. Doubling PSA changes the ramp, not simply today's annual rate. The native `prepayment01` bumps the parameter the model actually reads. For PSA that is a multiplier change corresponding to one basis point of terminal CPR.

CMBS lockout suppresses voluntary prepayment before the specified age. The controlled collateral here has a five-year bullet and a 60-month lockout, so local CPR sensitivity is zero over its life. Balloon refinancing, maturity default, collateral value and delayed recoveries still matter. The example explicitly charges master and special servicing fees on their native bases; a longer recovery lag can worsen liquidity even if nominal recovery is unchanged.

For RMBS, the supported shifting-interest rule gives the senior a scheduled share of **all** principal, with the balance allocated to other debt. It is a first-order non-agency treatment. An agency rule that splits scheduled principal and prepayments separately is more detailed than this input can express.

### A price quote and a first-loss threshold answer different questions

At 96.5, the BBB purchase amount is 96.5% of original tranche balance. The native metrics return model price and a quote-conditioned z-spread separately. `structured_credit_tranche_breakeven_cdr` finds the constant annual default rate at first principal writedown. It is not an economic break-even IRR and is not the CDR that makes the purchase NPV zero. Read the threshold together with the CPR×CDR×severity price and writedown table.

The program holds only the BBB tranche. It remains a separate analytical sleeve because the public native portfolio envelope represents the whole structured-credit deal. Adding that whole deal as though it were BBB would overstate the holding and double count other tranches. The reporting boundary therefore sums native portfolio value and exactly one separately priced BBB holding, with the same discipline under dates and stresses.

<Exercise id="c3-equity" title="Find where the equity investment fails" question="Build an equity CPR×CDR return grid and identify capital impairment. Reconcile each return to discounted cash before annualizing it.">

The public tranche table provides price, WAL and writedown, not a dated per-tranche payment ledger. We therefore use the explicitly reconciled **single-quarter deal** for an exact IRR exercise: its one payment equals native equity PV divided by its discount factor. This preserves an exact return calculation instead of mistaking five-year PV or WAL for an IRR. The five-year BBB scenario table above remains the long-horizon risk reference. Report holding-period return beside annualized IRR; a large annualized one-quarter number is not a sustainable equity forecast. Principal can be wiped out while a small interest distribution remains, so “capital dies” need not mean absolutely zero total cash.
