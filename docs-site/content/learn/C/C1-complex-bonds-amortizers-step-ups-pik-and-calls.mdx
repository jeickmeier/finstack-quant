<DeskContext question="How do cash coupons, PIK, amortization, and issuer calls interact in this 8NC3 bond?">

An 8NC3 has eight years to maturity and three years of call protection. This contract starts with 6% cash interest plus 2% PIK, steps its total coupon up 25 basis points after year four, begins repaying 1% of original face each quarter after year five, and becomes callable at 103 with a declining call price. Those terms define a changing principal balance and an exercise decision. A single yield does not describe either completely.

The required output is a validated contract, a cash-versus-capitalized-interest ladder, a model price, and a quote-conditioned YTW/OAS report. Explain the value given up to the issuer before proposing a trade.

</DeskContext>

<Standard>

### Start with the legal cashflows

For a quarter of year fraction `a`, beginning principal `B`, and total coupon `c`, a 75/25 split pays `B*c*0.75*a` in cash and capitalizes `B*c*0.25*a`. PIK is additional principal owed to the holder; it is not cash available to reinvest today. The following coupon is computed on the accreted balance. Scheduled principal repayments then reduce exposure. The fixture uses an Act/365F discount curve and the bond's own coupon accrual convention; do not replace either with a generic quarter in a production schedule.

Here the 25 bp step increases the *total* coupon from 8% to 8.25% while retaining the 75/25 split. A contract that keeps PIK at 2% and increases cash interest to 6.25% is a different instrument. The amortization is 1% of **original face**, not 1% of the PIK-accreted balance. A desk description must resolve both conventions.

A fixed-to-floating bond has one fixed window and a subsequent floating window. The switch date belongs to the schedule. A floor, reset lag, forward index and payment convention belong to the floating specification. Building the cashflows first makes an accidental overlapping or missing coupon window visible.

### Principal timing and issuer choice

Weighted average life is the principal-weighted time to repayment. It is not duration: coupon payments and discount rates do not enter the basic WAL calculation. A bullet, linear amortizer, fixed-percent sinking fund and custom repayment schedule can have identical face and maturity but different WAL. PIK can change the principal to be repaid, so the controlled WAL comparison below uses cash-only coupons.

A fixed-price call lets the issuer cap the holder's continuation value at the contractual redemption price. A make-whole clause instead derives redemption from remaining cashflows discounted at a reference curve plus a spread, subject to the call-price floor. The reference curve is an additional market dependency. The comparison below holds the call dates and price floors fixed and changes that protection alone.

YTM assumes redemption at maturity. YTW selects the least favorable eligible redemption yield for the holder at the supplied price. OAS solves a model-dependent spread after allowing for the embedded option. The `embedded_option_value` output uses the holder's signed callable-minus-straight convention. Interpret its sign before calling it an “option cost.” Neither OAS nor YTW is meaningful without identifying the observed-price assumption.

### Contractual PIK versus a PIK toggle

A split coupon is predetermined. A toggle gives the issuer a choice to defer cash interest, potentially when its credit quality is weakest. The structural Monte Carlo example monitors a simulated issuer state and elects PIK below a distance-to-default threshold. Asset value, debt barrier and bond notional must use a consistent money scale. Otherwise the apparent credit-quality experiment may merely compare incompatible units.

The native Merton path supports a fixed-rate bullet. It rejects this amortizing 8NC3. We therefore teach the same issuer-choice question on a matched, explicitly separate bullet bond and retain the cashflow/tree path for the full desk contract. The reported PIK fraction is a fraction of coupon decisions, not a probability of default. The seeded path results are model diagnostics, not an estimate calibrated to this issuer's actual behavior.

</Standard>

### Reconcile cash and PIK, then condition yield measures on a quote

Remove issuer exercise only in the contractual twin used to inspect scheduled payments. That twin answers what is owed if it remains outstanding. The callable model answers what the holder owns when exercise is allowed. The first two coupons test that PIK accretion carries into the next coupon base; the full table reconciles discounted cashflows.

{/* notebook-block notebook="02_pricing/instruments/complex_cashflows.ipynb" cell="pik-cash-ladder" role="build" */}
```python exec id=pik-cash-ladder role=build
from finstack_quant.valuations import instrument_cashflows
contractual=deepcopy(complex_payload)
contractual["instrument"]["spec"]["call_put"]=None
contractual["instrument"]["spec"]["id"]="PIK-CONTRACTUAL-TWIN"
flow_envelope,flows=instrument_cashflows(json.dumps(contractual),credit_market,AS_OF,model="discounting")
coupons=flows.loc[flows["kind"]=="fixed"].copy()
first_cash=1_000_000*.06*.25
first_pik=1_000_000*.02*.25
assert abs(coupons.iloc[0]["amount"]-first_cash)<.01
assert abs(coupons.iloc[1]["amount"]-(1_000_000+first_pik)*.06*.25)<.01
assert flow_envelope["reconciles_with_base_value"]
assert abs(flows["pv"].sum()-float(flow_envelope["total_pv"]))<.01
print(coupons[["date","amount","rate","pv"]].head(6))
```

### Build a fixed-to-floating schedule and compare principal rules

The native schedule builder emits fixed and floating cashflow kinds. The JSON bridge then turns that schedule into a priced bond. The printed initial funding is an issuance flow; the holder's as-of asset valuation excludes the already-funded initial exchange.

{/* notebook-block notebook="02_pricing/instruments/complex_cashflows.ipynb" cell="c1-fixed-float" role="build" */}

Each amortization case repays exactly the original cash-only principal. Read both that conservation check and WAL; an attractive shorter WAL is meaningless if principal has accidentally disappeared.

{/* notebook-block notebook="02_pricing/instruments/complex_cashflows.ipynb" cell="c1-amortization" role="build" */}
```python exec id=c1-amortization role=build
amortizations={"bullet":AmortizationSpec.NONE,
    "linear_to_zero":AmortizationSpec.linear_to(Money(0.,"USD")),
    "ten_percent_original":AmortizationSpec.percent_of_original_per_period(.10),
    "custom":AmortizationSpec.custom_principal([(date(2026,1,15),Money(400_000.,"USD"))])}
amort_rows=[]
for name,rule in amortizations.items():
    schedule=(CashFlowSchedule.builder().principal(Money(1_000_000.,"USD"),AS_OF,date(2027,1,15))
        .amortization(rule).fixed_cf(FixedCouponSpec(rate=Decimal("0.06"),schedule=ScheduleParams.quarterly_act360())).build())
    principal_flows=[f for f in schedule.get_flows() if f.date>AS_OF and f.kind in (CFKind.AMORTIZATION,CFKind.NOTIONAL)]
    paid=sum(f.amount.amount for f in principal_flows)
    wal=sum(f.amount.amount*(f.date-AS_OF).days/365 for f in principal_flows)/paid
    assert abs(paid-1_000_000)<.01
    amort_rows.append({"rule":name,"principal_paid":paid,"principal_WAL_years":wal})
amort_table=pd.DataFrame(amort_rows).set_index("rule")
assert amort_table.loc["linear_to_zero","principal_WAL_years"]<amort_table.loc["bullet","principal_WAL_years"]
print(amort_table)
```

### Price issuer toggle behavior on its supported model domain

The three cases change issuer asset coverage while holding volatility, debt barrier, recovery, bond terms, threshold and random seed fixed. Higher PIK frequency can support issuer liquidity while increasing the holder's exposure at default. Price and default frequency must therefore be read alongside the PIK statistic.

{/* notebook-block notebook="02_pricing/instruments/complex_cashflows.ipynb" cell="c1-toggle" role="build" */}
```python exec id=c1-toggle role=build
from finstack_quant.models.credit import MertonModel,ToggleExerciseModel
from finstack_quant.valuations.instruments import MertonMcConfig,PikMode,PikSchedule,BarrierCrossing
# Merton MC supports a bullet fixed-rate bond; it does not support the amortizing 8NC3.
toggle_bond=Bond.fixed("TOGGLE-BULLET",1_000_000.,.08,AS_OF,date(2030,1,15),"none","USD-OIS",currency="USD")
toggle_rows=[]
for asset_value in [900_000.,1_300_000.,1_800_000.]:
    config=(MertonMcConfig(MertonModel(asset_value,.25,800_000.,.04),.40).num_paths(2048).seed(20250115)
        .antithetic(True).time_steps_per_year(12).barrier_crossing(BarrierCrossing.discrete())
        .pik_schedule(PikSchedule.uniform(PikMode.toggle()))
        .toggle_model(ToggleExerciseModel.threshold("distance_to_default",2.,"below")))
    result=toggle_bond.price_merton_mc(config,.04,AS_OF)
    assert 0<=result.average_pik_fraction<=1
    assert result.average_pik_fraction==result.path_statistics.pik_exercise_rate
    toggle_rows.append({"asset_value":asset_value,"clean_price_pct":result.clean_price_pct,
        "PIK_fraction":result.average_pik_fraction,"default_rate":result.path_statistics.default_rate})
print(pd.DataFrame(toggle_rows))
```

{/* notebook-block notebook="02_pricing/instruments/complex_cashflows.ipynb" cell="c1-exercise-contract" role="exercise" */}
```python exec id=c1-exercise-contract role=exercise
# Start from the typed fixed-bond envelope and write every complex economic term.
answer=json.loads(Bond.fixed("ANALYST-PIK-CALLABLE",1_000_000.,.08,AS_OF,date(2033,1,15),
                            "short_front","USD-OIS",currency="USD").to_json())
a=answer["instrument"]["spec"]
coupon=a["cashflow_spec"]["fixed"];coupon.pop("rate")
coupon.update({"initial_rate":"0.08","step_schedule":[["2029-01-15","0.0825"]],
    "frequency":{"count":3,"unit":"months"},"day_count":"30_360",
    "business_day_convention":"following","calendar_id":"weekends_only","stub":"none",
    "end_of_month":False,"payment_lag_days":0,
    "coupon_type":{"split":{"cash_pct":"0.75","pik_pct":"0.25"}}})
amort_dates=[f"{year}-{month:02d}-15" for year in [2030,2031,2032]
             for month in [1,4,7,10] if (year,month)>(2030,1)]
a["cashflow_spec"]={"amortizing":{"base":{"step_up":coupon},"schedule":{
    "custom_principal":{"items":[[day,{"amount":"10000","currency":"USD"}] for day in amort_dates]}}}}
a["call_put"]={"puts":[],"calls":[{"start_date":f"{year}-01-15",
    "end_date":f"{year+1}-01-14" if year<2032 else "2033-01-15",
    "price_pct_of_par":call_price} for year,call_price in [(2028,103.),(2029,102.),(2030,101.),(2031,100.),(2032,100.)]]}
a["settlement_days"]=1
a["instrument_pricing_overrides"]={"market_quotes":{"implied_volatility":.01}}
answer_json=validate_instrument_json(json.dumps(answer))
answer_price=price_instrument(answer_json,credit_market,AS_OF).value.amount
assert abs(answer_price-callable_value)<.01
# Quote price and model value answer different questions.
a["instrument_pricing_overrides"]["market_quotes"]["quoted_clean_price"]=100.
answer_yields=price_instrument(json.dumps(answer),credit_market,AS_OF,metrics=["ytm","ytw","oas"])
```
