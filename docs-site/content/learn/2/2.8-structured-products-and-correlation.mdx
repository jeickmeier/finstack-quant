### Structured notes are bundles of contingent cash flows

An autocallable can terminate early when a reference level is reached. Conditional coupons, participation caps and a short downside option change the investor's exposure. The issuer's creditworthiness also matters: a contractual capital floor is an obligation of the issuer, not an external guarantee. The deterministic known-fixing example below isolates redemption and deliberately makes no issuer-credit valuation claim.

A TARN caps cumulative coupon payments at a target, so the last coupon may be truncated. A snowball coupon depends recursively on the previous coupon. An inverse floater decreases with its reference rate, subject to contractual limits. A range-accrual coupon multiplies a coupon rate by the fraction of qualifying observations and the accrual year fraction. Observation inclusivity and target units change cash; they are tested explicitly. [Overhaus](/references.html#overhaus-2007-equity-derivatives) supplies structured-equity context.

### Cash-flow tranching and loss tranching are different descriptions

A cash pool collects interest, scheduled principal, prepayments and recoveries. Fees and interest priorities precede principal distributions according to the deal rules. OC tests compare eligible collateral with specified debt; IC tests compare available interest with required interest. A failed test may redirect cash to senior repayment. Neither test is a substitute for modelling actual cash arrivals and recovery timing.

For constant annual rates, monthly mortality equivalents are

$$
\mathrm{SMM}=1-(1-\mathrm{CPR})^{1/12},\qquad
\mathrm{MDR}=1-(1-\mathrm{CDR})^{1/12}.
$$

CPR is prepayment, CDR is default; default severity determines lost principal, while recovery lag determines when the surviving recovery arrives. Constant curves are controlled scenarios. Mortgage seasoning, refinancing incentives and borrower heterogeneity require richer assumptions, as illustrated by [Richard–Roll](/references.html#richard-roll-1989) and [Schwartz–Torous](/references.html#schwartz-torous-1989).

A loss-only tranche with attachment $a$ and detachment $d$, expressed as fractions of pool notional $N$, absorbs

$$
L_{a,d}=N\min\{\max(L/N-a,0),d-a\}.
$$

The equity loss tranche attaches first at zero. Do not transfer those coordinates into the cash-stack builder without reading its contract: the native structured-credit fixture's senior `attachment_point`/`detachment_point` describe capital-stack ordering. Its AAA 0–80% coordinates are not an equity first-loss 0–80% tranche. The displayed copula experiment uses explicit loss attachment/detachment fractions in a separate object.

### Correlation changes joint outcomes while marginal default risk stays fixed

In the one-factor Gaussian model, name $i$ defaults when

$$
\sqrt\rho\,Z+\sqrt{1-\rho}\,\epsilon_i<\Phi^{-1}(p_i).
$$

Here $\rho$ is latent asset correlation, not observed default-indicator correlation. The API takes factor loadings, so equal-name loading is $\sqrt\rho$. Passing $\rho$ as the loading would produce the wrong dependence. With fixed marginal PD and LGD, expected total pool loss remains $\sum_i N_i p_i\mathrm{LGD}_i$; correlation changes its distribution and allocation across nonlinear tranches. [Li](/references.html#li-2000-gaussian-copula), [Vasicek](/references.html#vasicek-2002-loan-portfolio) and [Laurent–Gregory](/references.html#laurent-gregory-2005-factor-copulas) develop this modelling family.

Conditioning on the common factor makes defaults independent, with conditional PD

$$
p_i(z)=\Phi\left(\frac{\Phi^{-1}(p_i)-\sqrt\rho z}{\sqrt{1-\rho}}\right).
$$

For a homogeneous infinitely granular pool, conditional loss converges to LGD times this conditional PD: the Vasicek loan-loss limit. Finite pools retain idiosyncratic granularity. Factor-copula semi-analytics integrate conditional loss distributions over the common factor; conditional recursion/convolution or quadrature replaces part of the brute-force sampling. The native experiment below uses finite-pool Monte Carlo, so its sampling error must still be reported.

Base correlation quotes cumulative zero-to-detachment tranches. A tranche's expected loss can be represented as the difference of two such cumulative pieces. Different detachment correlations fit different quotes; the resulting surface is not automatically one coherent joint default model. Check loss monotonicity across detachments and time, interpolation behaviour and quote fit. A bootstrap proceeds through quoted maturities and detachments, solving a correlation to reproduce each spread/upfront quote while keeping the marginal credit and discount inputs consistent. Check each residual after interpolation and test the implied cumulative expected-loss surface. This lesson reprices supplied base-correlation curves; it does not label that sweep a completed bootstrap. Calibration identifies parameters within the chosen convention, not the true dependence law. [Gibson](/references.html#gibson-2004-synthetic-cdos), [Hull–White](/references.html#hull-white-2004-cdo) and [Krekel–Stumpp](/references.html#krekel-stumpp-2006-correlation-products) provide the tranche-pricing context.

### 1. Read redemption and coupon arithmetic in cash units

The autocallable experiment supplies observed fixings, zero discount rates and zero coupons, and sets the call threshold out of reach. This removes unrelated cash flows while testing capital floors, participation caps and the investor's short knock-in put. A breach followed by complete spot recovery returns principal in this contract: touching a barrier does not mean principal is automatically lost.

{/* notebook-block notebook="07_advanced_quant/monte_carlo/exotic_payoffs_and_pricers.ipynb" cell="structured-note-redemption" role="build" */}
```python exec id=structured-note-redemption role=build
import datetime as dt
import json
from finstack_quant.core.market_data import DiscountCurve, MarketContext
from finstack_quant.core.money import Money
from finstack_quant.valuations.instruments import price_instrument

note_market = MarketContext().insert(DiscountCurve.flat("USD-OIS", dt.date(2026, 1, 1), 0.0))
note_market.insert_price("SPX-SPOT", Money(100.0, "USD"))
note_spec = {
    "id": "NOTE-PAYOFF-PROOF", "underlying_ticker": "SPX",
    "observation_dates": ["2025-12-30", "2025-12-31"],
    "payment_dates": ["2026-01-02", "2026-01-02"], "expiry": "2026-01-02",
    "autocall_barriers": [3.0, 3.0], "coupon_barriers": [0.0, 0.0],
    "coupons": [0.0, 0.0], "memory_coupons": False,
    "final_barrier": 0.7, "participation_rate": 1.0, "cap_level": 1.5,
    "notional": {"amount": "1000", "currency": "USD"}, "day_count": "act_365f",
    "discount_curve_id": "USD-OIS", "spot_id": "SPX-SPOT", "vol_surface_id": "SPX-VOL",
    "path_model": "atm_term_gbm", "div_yield_id": None, "initial_level": 100.0,
    "attributes": {},
}
print("terminal | capital floor/cap | 50% upside | knock-in principal")
for terminal in (50.0, 70.0, 90.0, 100.0, 125.0, 160.0):
    note_spec["past_fixings"] = [["2025-12-30", 100.0], ["2025-12-31", terminal]]
    ratio = terminal / 100.0
    contracts = [
        ({"capital_protection": {"floor": 1.0}}, max(1.0, min(ratio, 1.5))),
        ({"participation": {"rate": 0.5}}, 1.0 + 0.5 * max(min(ratio, 1.5) - 1.0, 0.0)),
        ({"knock_in_put": {"strike": 100.0}}, ratio if ratio <= 0.7 else 1.0),
    ]
    values = []
    for payoff, expected in contracts:
        note_spec["final_payoff_type"] = payoff
        envelope = {"schema": "finstack_quant.instrument/1",
                    "instrument": {"type": "autocallable", "spec": note_spec}}
        value = price_instrument(json.dumps(envelope), note_market, "2026-01-01").price
        assert abs(value - 1000 * expected) < 1e-10
        values.append(value)
    print(f"{terminal:8.0f} | {values[0]:17.2f} | {values[1]:10.2f} | {values[2]:18.2f}")
note_spec["past_fixings"] = [["2025-12-30", 50.0], ["2025-12-31", 100.0]]
envelope["instrument"]["spec"] = note_spec
recovered = price_instrument(json.dumps(envelope), note_market, "2026-01-01").price
assert recovered == 1000.0
print(f"Prior knock-in with full recovery: {recovered:.2f}")
```

### 3. Hold PD and LGD fixed; change only dependence

The pool now has 125 equal names, USD 100 million notional, 5% horizon PD and 60% LGD. Its analytical expected loss is USD 3 million under both asset correlations. Each simulation checks that expectation within four estimated SE. The tranche losses are computed both by the native result and by explicit clipping; their sums reconcile to total portfolio loss within USD 0.000001.

{/* notebook-block notebook="07_advanced_quant/correlation_and_credit_models.ipynb" cell="copula-tranche-losses" role="build" */}
```python exec id=copula-tranche-losses role=build
import math
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from finstack_quant.models.correlation import (
    CopulaSpec, CreditExposure, PortfolioLossConfig, LatentSingleFactor, simulate_portfolio_loss,
)

latent = LatentSingleFactor(0.15, 0.5)
assert latent.num_factors == 1  # Descriptor, not an injected simulation engine.
pool_notional = 100_000_000.0
tranche_bounds = [("equity", 0.0, 0.03), ("mezz", 0.03, 0.15), ("senior", 0.15, 1.0)]
correlation_rows, loss_samples = [], {}
for rho in (0.1, 0.5):
    exposures = [CreditExposure(f"NAME-{i:03}", pool_notional / 125, 0.05, 0.6, [math.sqrt(rho)])
                 for i in range(125)]
    result = simulate_portfolio_loss(exposures, PortfolioLossConfig(16384, 17, 0.99, CopulaSpec.gaussian()))
    losses = np.asarray(result.losses)
    loss_samples[rho] = losses / pool_notional
    standard_error = losses.std(ddof=1) / math.sqrt(len(losses))
    assert abs(result.expected_loss - pool_notional * 0.05 * 0.6) < 4 * standard_error
    allocated = 0.0
    for name, attachment, detachment in tranche_bounds:
        stats = result.tranche_loss_statistics(attachment, detachment, pool_notional)
        hand = np.clip(losses / pool_notional - attachment, 0, detachment - attachment) * pool_notional
        assert abs(hand.mean() - stats.expected_loss_amount) < 1e-6
        allocated += stats.expected_loss_amount
        correlation_rows.append({"rho": rho, "tranche": name, "expected_loss_usd": stats.expected_loss_amount,
                                 "expected_principal_usd": (detachment - attachment) * pool_notional - stats.expected_loss_amount})
    assert abs(allocated - result.expected_loss) < 1e-6
correlation_table = pd.DataFrame(correlation_rows)
print(correlation_table.to_string(index=False))
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 3.6))
for rho, values in loss_samples.items():
    ax.hist(values * 100, bins=np.arange(0, 62, 2), density=True, histtype="step", linewidth=2, label=f"Asset correlation {rho:.0%}")
ax.set(xlabel="Pool loss (% of notional)", ylabel="Density per percentage point", title="Same marginal PD and LGD; different joint tails")
ax.legend(); fig.tight_layout()

loss_stack = correlation_table.pivot(index="rho", columns="tranche", values="expected_loss_usd")[["equity","mezz","senior"]] / 1e6
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8,3.6))
loss_stack.plot.bar(stacked=True,ax=ax,color=["#a45227","#75b2a4","#154c53"],rot=0)
ax.set(xlabel="Latent asset correlation",ylabel="Expected loss (USD millions)",title="Tranche losses sum to the same expected pool loss within sampling error")
ax.legend(title="Loss tranche");fig.tight_layout()
```

<Exercise title="3. What does the Gaussian copula leave out?" question="Write a short credit-committee explanation of why a Gaussian correlation fit is insufficient evidence of protection against a crisis such as 2008. Separate assumptions, calibration and structural risks.">

The single-factor construction specifies a static dependence shape, fixed marginal default probabilities and the chosen recovery assumptions. A Gaussian copula has no asymptotic tail dependence at correlations strictly below one; a fitted moderate correlation can underrepresent joint extremes if the actual dependence changes in stress. Common funding, liquidity, underwriting and macroeconomic exposures can be stronger than the historical sample suggests. Default probabilities and recoveries may deteriorate together, violating a fixed-PD/fixed-LGD stress interpretation.

A good fit to tranche quotes only shows that the chosen parameters reproduce those quotes. Base-correlation interpolation may fail loss or time monotonicity, and a surface of separate quoted correlations need not define one joint law. Small historical samples, unstable regimes and model-dependent marks weaken the inference from fit to economic safety.

The deal can also lose value through trigger diversion, delayed recovery, prepayment changes, financing pressure, issuer or counterparty default and illiquidity. Those channels are not repaired by changing one copula parameter. The appropriate response is a transparent marginal/default/recovery stress set, dependence alternatives, explicit cash-flow and trigger analysis, and a record of which risks each model excludes.
