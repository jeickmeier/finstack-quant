<DeskContext question={"Rates sold off 25bp. Estimate our bond P&L in ten seconds \u2014 then explain why the estimate was off."}>

The PM asks what a 25bp rates selloff will cost, and whether the bond's spread risk is the same thing. A single duration number cannot answer both questions. You need the shock definition, the currency, the curve or spread factor, the bump size and the local approximation error.

You will reconcile native signed DV01 to direct repricing, explain the quadratic improvement from convexity, and read a qualified key-rate report. You will also compare the spread measures that traders use to describe the same bond under different benchmarks.

</DeskContext>

<Standard>

### Duration is a local derivative

Macaulay duration is a PV-weighted average cashflow time under the yield convention. For a regular instrument with $m$ payments per year, modified duration is $D_{\mathrm{mod}}=D_{\mathrm{Mac}}/(1+y/m)$ and approximates the fractional price decline for a small increase in yield. The native metric names are `duration_mac` and `duration_mod`.

With decimal yield shock $\Delta y$,

$$\frac{\Delta P}{P}\approx-D_{\mathrm{mod}}\Delta y+\tfrac12 C_y(\Delta y)^2,\qquad C_y=\frac1P\frac{\partial^2P}{\partial y^2}.$$

The bond's native `convexity` metric is $C_y/100$ on this API. Multiply it by 100 before using the decimal-yield formula above. The output explicitly prints both representations. A scalar that is numerically plausible can still be wrong by a factor of 100 if its units are assumed.

Curve DV01 and yield duration are related but not identical. A yield shock shifts the single rate that reproduces the bond quote; a curve shock shifts market discounting and potentially projection inputs. Here `dv01` is the **signed currency price change for +1bp**, using a central bump where supported. A long fixed-rate bond has negative DV01. Desks sometimes quote its positive loss magnitude; state the sign convention before applying a hedge ratio. Dollar duration per unit decimal yield is about 10,000 times signed DV01, with the appropriate sign.

The direct curve experiment multiplies discount factors by $e^{-\Delta r t}$, so it is a parallel **continuous zero-rate** shift. At +25bp, a first-order estimate is $25\times DV01$. The second difference at ±1bp gives a curvature term in dollars per bp squared. Using that same shock coordinate makes the linear and quadratic comparisons consistent. Convexity improves this fixture's approximation but does not make a large nonlinear shock exact. A residual may contain third-order effects, recalibration changes, option exercise, cashflow changes or inconsistent conventions; it is not automatically model error.

### Buckets identify the factor

A qualified key such as `bucketed_dv01::USD-OIS::5y` carries the measure, curve and tenor. The curve's input pillars and the risk report's key-rate grid are different objects. Native triangular key-rate bumps distribute a local shock across neighboring tenors; the resulting bond exposure can span several buckets. A seven-year bond on a curve with 2, 5, 10 and 30-year pillars is not simply a seven-year point exposure. Coupons occur earlier and interpolation couples cashflows to nearby curve regions.

Check the actual returned keys and retain their curve and currency. Bucket sums can differ slightly from a parallel shock because finite differences, bump shape and interpolation differ. Do not force an exact equality by moving a residual into one bucket. [Tuckman and Serrat](/references.html#tuckman-serrat-fixed-income) develop the risk interpretation; the exercise prints the native keys rather than presupposing them.

### Several spreads, several questions

| Measure | Benchmark and meaning | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G-spread | Bond yield minus same-currency government benchmark yield | Yield conventions and benchmark maturity must match |
| I-spread | Bond yield minus interpolated par swap rate | A single yield comparison does not reprice each cashflow |
| Z-spread | Constant spread applied to the discounting zero curve to reproduce price | Assumes fixed cashflows; compounding convention matters |
| Asset-swap spread | Spread on a floating leg after exchanging the bond's fixed economics | Depends on par/market asset-swap structure, annuities and settlement |
| Discount margin | Spread measure for a floating-rate note relative to projected index cashflows | Depends on resets, index projection and discount assumptions |
| OAS | Spread after modelling embedded exercise options | Depends on volatility, dynamics and exercise assumptions |

The library returns I-spread, Z-spread and asset-swap spread as **decimal rates**; the display converts them to basis points. There is no native `g_spread` metric here. We visibly subtract an assumed 4% USD government benchmark from the USD bond's yield. Subtracting the EUR government bond's yield would mix currencies and would not be a G-spread. A production benchmark should also match maturity and yield convention.

`asw_par` constructs settlement-to-maturity swap legs, which need a stub when settlement is off cycle. The example uses a fresh `short_front` copy for that calculation. This convention choice belongs in the analysis, rather than a hidden workaround. `discount_margin` is shown on a separate synthetic floating-rate bond, whose projection curve and fixings are supplied by the rates-stage market.

### Spread risk is not one universal bump

For this corporate bond with no hazard curve, `cs01::USD-CORP` is the forward difference $P(z+1\mathrm{bp})-P(z)$, with the spread added to periodically compounded zero rates and prices anchored at settlement. It differs from the continuous curve DV01 experiment. The exercise reproduces it through `quoted_z_spread` and also prints the central difference, exposing the small curvature difference. Bond `z_spread` itself is decimal, not basis points.

A CDS backed by a calibrated hazard curve instead reports quote-space `cs01::ACME-HZD`: the market CDS quotes move and the survival curve is rebuilt. It is a different risk factor, even though both outputs are dollars per basis point. Aggregating two numbers with the same unit does not make them an economic hedge.

For a callable bond, the issuer's option reduces investor value relative to an otherwise identical straight bond. Positive rate volatility generally increases that option's value. A model can report embedded-option value and solve OAS, but neither should be inferred by subtracting two unrelated yield spreads. Callable effective duration must also allow exercise to change under a shock; C1 works through that model-dependent extension.

### Reprice a parallel curve shock

This creates independent bumped markets, reconciles a native ±1bp derivative and compares actual +25bp P&L with first- and second-order estimates. It also verifies that the full curve-qualified bucket keys are available.

{/* notebook-block notebook="02_pricing/instruments/bonds_and_fixed_income.ipynb" cell="bond-risk-proof" role="build" */}
```python exec id=bond-risk-proof role=build
import copy, json, math
from finstack_quant.core.market_data import DiscountCurve, MarketContext
from finstack_quant.valuations.instruments import price_instrument
from _shared.analyst_book import AS_OF, build_market, instruments

market = build_market('foundations')
curve = market.get_discount('USD-OIS')
analysis_bond = instruments('foundations')['USD-CORP']
# ASW constructs a new settlement-to-maturity leg, which has a short front stub.
analysis_bond['instrument']['spec']['cashflow_spec']['fixed']['stub'] = 'short_front'
bond = json.dumps(analysis_bond)
result = price_instrument(bond, market, AS_OF, metrics=['dv01', 'bucketed_dv01', 'duration_mod', 'convexity', 'cs01', 'z_spread', 'i_spread', 'asw_par'])
values = {}
for bp in (-1.0, 0.0, 1.0, 25.0):
    bumped = MarketContext.from_json(market.to_json())
    bumped.insert(DiscountCurve('USD-OIS', AS_OF,
        [(t, df * math.exp(-bp * 1e-4 * t)) for t, df in zip(curve.knots, curve.dfs)], interp='log_linear'))
    values[bp] = price_instrument(bond, bumped, AS_OF).price
dv01 = (values[1.0] - values[-1.0]) / 2  # native signed change for +1 bp
assert abs(dv01 - result.get_metric('dv01')) < 0.01
actual = values[25.0] - values[0.0]
linear = 25 * dv01
quadratic = linear + 0.5 * 25 ** 2 * (values[1.0] + values[-1.0] - 2 * values[0.0])
assert abs(actual - quadratic) < abs(actual - linear)
assert result.get_metric('cs01::USD-CORP') is not None
assert any(key.startswith('bucketed_dv01::USD-OIS::') for key in result.metric_keys())
print({'actual_pnl': actual, 'linear': linear, 'quadratic': quadratic, 'yield_convexity': result.get_metric('convexity')})
```

### Compare the spread ladder

A 99 quote makes the price-based spread comparison nontrivial. The government yield is an explicit illustrative USD input. The floating-rate note uses an independent native fixture with the required SOFR curve and historical fixings.

{/* notebook-block notebook="02_pricing/instruments/bonds_and_fixed_income.ipynb" cell="bond-spread-ladder" role="build" */}
```python exec id=bond-spread-ladder role=build
import copy,json
from finstack_quant.valuations.instruments import price_instrument
from _shared.analyst_book import AS_OF,build_market,instruments
from _shared.instrument_fixtures import floating_bond,instrument_envelope
market=build_market('rates')
corporate=instruments('foundations')['USD-CORP']
corporate['instrument']['spec']['cashflow_spec']['fixed']['stub']='short_front'
corporate['instrument']['spec']['instrument_pricing_overrides']={'market_quotes':{'quoted_clean_price':99.0}}
r=price_instrument(json.dumps(corporate),market,AS_OF,metrics=['ytm','i_spread','z_spread','asw_par'])
# A same-currency synthetic government yield is an explicit benchmark, not a native g_spread metric.
government_yield=0.04
g_spread_bp=(r.get_metric('ytm')-government_yield)*1e4
_,frn=floating_bond(0)
frn_result=price_instrument(json.dumps(instrument_envelope(frn)),market,AS_OF,metrics=['discount_margin'])
assert all(r.get_metric(k) is not None for k in ['i_spread','z_spread','asw_par'])
print({'government_yield_decimal':government_yield,'G_spread_bp':g_spread_bp,'I_spread_bp':1e4*r.get_metric('i_spread'),'Z_spread_bp':1e4*r.get_metric('z_spread'),'ASW_par_bp':1e4*r.get_metric('asw_par'),'FRN_discount_margin_decimal':frn_result.get_metric('discount_margin')})
```

<Exercise id="exercise-zspread-cs01" title="B. Reproduce z-spread CS01" question={"Reprice the bond at z\u22121bp, z and z+1bp, and reconcile native CS01 to its documented one-sided convention."}>

{/* notebook-block notebook="02_pricing/instruments/bonds_and_fixed_income.ipynb" cell="exercise-zspread-cs01" role="exercise" */}

<Exercise id="exercise-amortizer-duration" title="C. Explain earlier principal repayment" question={"Build a native amortizer with the bullet coupon and maturity, then compare modified durations."}>

The native principal schedule returns cash earlier, reducing later interest-bearing principal. In this fixture its modified duration is shorter than the bullet. This is a cashflow timing explanation, not a blanket claim that every amortizing security has low risk under all prepayment or extension scenarios.

{/* notebook-block notebook="02_pricing/instruments/bonds_and_fixed_income.ipynb" cell="exercise-amortizer-duration" role="exercise" */}
```python exec id=exercise-amortizer-duration role=exercise
import json
from datetime import date
from decimal import Decimal
from finstack_quant.cashflows.builder import CashFlowSchedule, FixedCouponSpec, ScheduleParams, AmortizationSpec
from finstack_quant.core.money import Money
from finstack_quant.valuations.instruments import bond_from_cashflows_json, price_instrument
from _shared.analyst_book import AS_OF, build_market, instruments

market = build_market('foundations')
schedule = (CashFlowSchedule.builder().principal(Money(2_000_000.0, 'USD'), date(2024, 1, 15), date(2030, 1, 15))
    .amortization(AmortizationSpec.linear_to(Money(0.0, 'USD')))
    .fixed_cf(FixedCouponSpec(Decimal('0.055'), ScheduleParams.semiannual_30360()))
    .build())
amortizer = bond_from_cashflows_json('LESSON-AMORTIZER', schedule.to_json(), 'USD-OIS')
amortized = price_instrument(amortizer, market, AS_OF, metrics=['duration_mod', 'ytm'])
bullet = price_instrument(json.dumps(instruments('foundations')['USD-CORP']), market, AS_OF, metrics=['duration_mod'])
assert amortized.price > 0
assert amortized.get_metric('duration_mod') < bullet.get_metric('duration_mod')
print({'amortizer_duration': amortized.get_metric('duration_mod'), 'bullet_duration': bullet.get_metric('duration_mod')})
```

<CheckYourself>

1. **A long bond has DV01 = −930. What does +25bp suggest?** About USD −23,250 at first order, before convexity and other factors.
2. **Why does the bucket key include a curve ID?** USD discount risk and another curve's projection or credit risk are not interchangeable.
3. **What is wrong with putting native convexity directly into a decimal-yield Taylor equation?** This metric is scaled by 1/100; undo that scale first.
4. **Are I-spread and Z-spread the same when the curve slopes?** Generally no: one compares yields, while the other reprices the entire cashflow vector.
5. **Can bond z-spread CS01 cancel CDS quote CS01 without more assumptions?** Only under a stated joint spread scenario; the factor names must remain visible.
