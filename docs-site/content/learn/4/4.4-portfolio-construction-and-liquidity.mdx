<DeskContext question={"Given our views and limits, what should we hold, and how quickly could we get out?"}>

The common book is fully priceable, but priceability does not establish a sensible allocation or an executable exit. We optimize a proportional copy of its eight-contract universe, inspect its risk-budget concentration, then examine trading capacity and spread/impact costs. The data are synthetic and every capacity assumption is visible.

Complete three sessions: constraints and allocation, risk budgets, then liquidity and execution. An unavailable loan capacity remains unavailable throughout the exercise.

</DeskContext>

<Standard>

Expected-return estimates and covariances are noisy. An unconstrained optimum can concentrate in the most optimistic estimate, so the constraint set is part of the investment decision. The current native optimizer is a linear-programming engine with explicit objectives and constraints; the example maximizes estimated annual profit subject to a unit budget, bounds and turnover. It is not presented as a quadratic mean-variance optimizer. We use the mean-variance lens separately to discuss estimation fragility and measure covariance risk.

The shared daily panel is modeled P&L divided by fixed risk capital, not actual book returns. Assign USD100,000 of risk capital to one full contract for this controlled allocation exercise. Scale every initial holding to 0.125 units so the eight quantities sum to one; this preserves relative holdings while giving an explicit USD100,000 total risk-capital budget. The sum-to-one native budget applies to contract quantities, not net market value. This avoids dividing derivative returns by a near-zero PV. The real one-unit holdings remain unchanged in the factory.

A 99% one-day Gaussian VaR decomposition uses aligned covariance and currency exposures. Component VaRs must sum to portfolio VaR by Euler homogeneity; hedges can have negative contributions. Native loss values use a negative sign. Equal target risk shares are a stated comparison, not a claim that equal capital produces equal risk.

Trading capacity must use the same units as position quantity. The fixture records full-contract units per day and separately gives notional per unit. OTC capacities are invented assumptions. Cash deposits have contractual treatment; the loan has no available capacity estimate. Neither is silently assigned a zero liquidation horizon. Bangia LVaR adds a stressed half-spread cost to market loss under its model assumptions. Apply its cash-price spread convention only to the two cash bonds here; OTC derivative closeout and unquoted-loan liquidity need additional data and conventions.

Roll uses negative serial covariance as a bid-ask-spread proxy; it may be unestimable when that condition fails. Amihud relates absolute returns to dollar volume. The native Kyle estimate relates price moves to volume under its stated proxy convention; it is not a causal market-impact guarantee. Almgren–Chriss shows the tension between slower trading's lower temporary impact and higher exposure to price risk. All these measures depend on sampling, units and market regime.

</Standard>

{/* notebook-block notebook="05_portfolio/liquidity_risk.ipynb" cell="constrained-common-universe" role="build" */}
```python exec id=constrained-common-universe role=build
import json
import pandas as pd
from _shared.analyst_book import AS_OF,book_spec,build_market
from finstack_quant.portfolio import (Objective,MetricExpr,PerPositionMetric,PortfolioOptimizationSpec,WeightingScheme,Constraint,PositionFilter,MissingMetricPolicy,optimize_portfolio)
# A proportional1/8 copy of the common book. Unit quantities sum to1.
# Each full contract is assigned100k USD fixed risk capital for the proxy panel.
opt_spec=book_spec("common")
position_ids=[p["position_id"] for p in opt_spec["positions"]]
expected_profit={pid:float(panel[pid].mean()*252*100_000) for pid in position_ids}
for p in opt_spec["positions"]:
 p["quantity"]=.125
 p["attributes"]={"expected_profit_usd":expected_profit[p["position_id"]]}
objective=Objective.maximize(MetricExpr.weighted_sum(PerPositionMetric.attribute("expected_profit_usd")))
def optimize_units(cap,turnover=None):
 spec=PortfolioOptimizationSpec.new(json.dumps(opt_spec),objective).with_weighting(WeightingScheme.unit_scaling()).with_missing_metric_policy(MissingMetricPolicy.strict()).with_constraint(Constraint.weight_bounds(PositionFilter.all(),0.,cap,label="position_cap"))
 # Native optimization includes one sum(weights)=1 budget; don't add a duplicate.
 if turnover is not None:spec=spec.with_constraint(Constraint.max_turnover(turnover,label="turnover"))
 result=optimize_portfolio(spec,build_market("common"))
 assert result.is_feasible and abs(sum(result.optimal_weights.values())-1)<1e-8
 assert all(-1e-10<=w<=cap+1e-8 for w in result.optimal_weights.values())
 if turnover is not None:assert result.turnover<=turnover+1e-8
 assert abs(result.objective_value-sum(expected_profit[pid]*w for pid,w in result.optimal_weights.items()))<1e-6
 return result
corner=optimize_units(1.)
constrained=optimize_units(.35,.40)
assert corner.objective_value>=constrained.objective_value-1e-8
print(pd.DataFrame({"expected_annual_profit_per_full_contract_USD":expected_profit,"initial_units":{pid:.125 for pid in position_ids},"corner_units":corner.optimal_weights,"constrained_units":constrained.optimal_weights}).to_string())
print({"corner_objective_usd":corner.objective_value,"constrained_objective_usd":constrained.objective_value,"sum_absolute_quantity_changes":constrained.turnover,"binding":constrained.binding_constraints})
```

### Session 2: inspect who spends risk

Use the optimized quantities times their risk-capital scale as currency exposures against the same aligned covariance matrix. The component sum closes within USD1e-8. A position with a small allocation may consume a large risk share because of its volatility and covariance. A binding allocation constraint and a breached risk target describe different limits.

### Session 3: quantify liquidation costs with matching units

The cash-bond LVaR table retains native model loss, spread cost and adjusted loss separately. Other instrument types are explicitly outside this particular spread-cost calculation. The microstructure example uses shares for Kyle and share-volume times USD 50 for Amihud. In the impact example both order size and ADV are in shares; the reference price makes cost scaling explicit.

{/* notebook-block notebook="05_portfolio/liquidity_risk.ipynb" cell="risk-budget-and-liquidity" role="build" */}
```python exec id=risk-budget-and-liquidity role=build
from finstack_quant.models.factor.risk import parametric_var_decomposition,evaluate_risk_budget
from finstack_quant.models.liquidity import lvar_bangia,roll_effective_spread,amihud_illiquidity,almgren_chriss_impact,kyle_lambda
from finstack_quant.portfolio import value_portfolio
from finstack_quant.analytics import Performance
covariance=panel[position_ids].cov().to_numpy()
# Currency exposure is position quantity times the disclosed100k proxy-risk-capital denominator.
exposure=[100_000*constrained.optimal_weights[pid] for pid in position_ids]
decomp=parametric_var_decomposition(position_ids,exposure,covariance,confidence=.99,compute_incremental=True)
components=[x.component_var for x in decomp.var_contributions]
assert abs(sum(components)-decomp.portfolio_var)<1e-8
budget=evaluate_risk_budget(position_ids,components,[1/8]*8,decomp.portfolio_var)
print(pd.DataFrame([{"position":x.position_id,"component_VaR_USD":x.component_var,"share":x.relative_var} for x in decomp.var_contributions]).to_string(index=False))
print("Risk-budget report:",budget.to_json())
# LVaR's price-spread formula is applied to the cash bonds only.
# OTC derivative closeout spreads and the unquoted loan need additional conventions/data.
values=json.loads(value_portfolio(json.dumps(book_spec("common")),build_market("common"),metrics=[]).to_json())
lvar_rows=[]
for pid in ["USD-CORP","EUR-GOVT"]:
 pv=float(values["position_values"][pid]["value_base"]["amount"])
 returns_frame=panel[[pid]]
 proxy=Performance.from_returns(returns_frame,frequency="daily")
 var=proxy.parametric_var(.99).iloc[0]*100_000
 spread=float(liquidity.loc[pid,"spread_fraction"])
 result=lvar_bangia(var,spread,spread*.25,.99,pv)
 assert result.lvar<=result.var and result.spread_cost>=0
 lvar_rows.append({"position":pid,"market_var_USD":result.var,"spread_cost_USD":result.spread_cost,"LVaR_USD":result.lvar,"PV_USD":pv})
print(pd.DataFrame(lvar_rows).to_string(index=False))
# Visible synthetic microstructure panel; quantity is shares, dollar volume is shares*price.
rng=np.random.default_rng(44);micro_r=.0001+.001*(-1.)**np.arange(252)+rng.normal(0,.00025,252)
share_volume=rng.uniform(80_000,120_000,252);reference_price=50.0
dollar_volume=share_volume*reference_price
roll=roll_effective_spread(micro_r.tolist())
amihud=amihud_illiquidity(micro_r.tolist(),dollar_volume.tolist())
kyle=kyle_lambda(micro_r.tolist(),share_volume.tolist(),reference_price)
assert roll is not None and roll>0 and amihud is not None and amihud>0 and kyle is not None
print({"Roll_relative_spread":roll,"Amihud_return_per_USD":amihud,"Kyle_USD_per_share_of_volume":kyle})
impact_rows=[]
for horizon in [1.,5.,10.]:
 estimate=almgren_chriss_impact(10_000.,100_000.,.02,horizon,.1,.2,reference_price=50.)
 impact_rows.append({"horizon_days":horizon,"temporary_impact":estimate.temporary_impact,"cost_USD":estimate.total_cost,"cost_bp":estimate.cost_bp,"execution_risk":estimate.execution_risk})
assert impact_rows[-1]["temporary_impact"]<impact_rows[0]["temporary_impact"]
assert impact_rows[-1]["execution_risk"]>impact_rows[0]["execution_risk"]
print(pd.DataFrame(impact_rows).to_string(index=False))
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
pd.DataFrame(impact_rows).set_index("horizon_days")[["cost_USD","execution_risk"]].plot(title="Synthetic execution: cost and one-sigma risk",ylabel="USD")
plt.tight_layout()
```

<ExecutedOutput lesson="4.4" block="risk-budget-and-liquidity" />

The chart reports trading cost and execution risk in USD under fixed illustrative coefficients. Slower execution reduces the temporary-impact component and raises execution risk in this model. Capacity during stress can deteriorate at the same time as spreads widen; the static estimates are not a promise of realizable liquidation proceeds.
