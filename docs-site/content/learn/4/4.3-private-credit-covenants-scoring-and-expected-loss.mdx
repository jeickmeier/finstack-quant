### Contract definitions come before ratios

A maintenance covenant is tested on an agreed schedule; an incurrence covenant restricts specified actions. A springing test activates only when its contractual condition holds. Cov-lite means fewer maintenance protections, not absence of risk or a guarantee of payment. Cure rights, waivers, baskets, EBITDA add-backs, netting rules and consequences must come from the agreement.

The fixture uses net debt divided by current-quarter EBITDA times four, capped at 4.5×. Its DSCR is EBITDA less cash taxes, capex and working-capital investment, divided by cash interest plus mandatory amortization; the minimum is 1.1×. This is the fixture's definition, not a universal DSCR formula. Trailing EBITDA would give a different leverage result.

For an upper-bound test, headroom in ratio units is limit minus actual. For a lower-bound test it is actual minus limit. The native report divides that difference by the threshold, returning a **fraction of the limit**. A value of −0.05 therefore means a 5% shortfall against the threshold, not a shortfall of 0.05×. The code prints both conventions and reconciles them. Test-frequency metadata is descriptive; the caller supplies the actual test dates.

### Accounting scores, rating scales and structural signals

Altman Z combines working capital, retained earnings, EBIT, equity value and sales ratios. Its original public-manufacturer, private-company Z-prime and non-manufacturer Z-double-prime variants use different specifications. Ohlson uses a logit-style score and Zmijewski a probit-style score. Sample applicability, financial-statement definitions, size units, price-level normalization and estimation vintage affect interpretation. A formula output is not automatically a calibrated probability for this borrower. [Altman](/references.html#altman-1968), [Ohlson](/references.html#ohlson-1980) and [Zmijewski](/references.html#zmijewski-1984) are the original model references.

The private borrower has no observed listed market capitalization or complete current-liability split in the compact fixture. Those extra inputs are explicitly assumed below. A master scale maps supplied PDs or probability-bearing score results into configured grade bands. It does not independently validate the underlying score or assign an agency rating. [Rating-symbol definitions](/references.html#moodys-rating-symbols) are context, not a substitute for an issuer rating process.

PIT PD conditions default risk on a current macro state; TTC PD averages through the cycle under a model convention. The one-factor transformation uses a specified asset correlation and systematic state. A negative state is adverse in this implementation. Its algebraic inverse provides a consistency check, not evidence that the estimated cycle state is correct. Physical underwriting PD and quote-implied risk-neutral default probabilities should remain labelled separately.

### Migration is not linear PD scaling

A rating transition matrix has nonnegative rows summing to one, with default absorbing. Under a time-homogeneous Markov model, $P(t)=\exp(Qt)$, where generator rows sum to zero and off-diagonal intensities are nonnegative. Matrix logarithms do not make every empirical transition matrix embeddable. The native Israel–Rosenthal–Wei extraction includes regularization diagnostics and a reconstruction error; inspect both rather than silently accepting a repaired matrix. [Israel–Rosenthal–Wei](/references.html#israel-rosenthal-wei-2001) and [Lando–Skødeberg](/references.html#lando-skodeberg-2002) provide the methodological context.

Cumulative default probability at five years is an entry of $P(5)$, not five times the one-year probability. Marginal unconditional bucket default probability is the difference between successive cumulative probabilities. Summing conditional annual PDs without survival weighting double-counts names that have already defaulted.

### Recovery and accounting loss use explicit horizons

Workout LGD deducts haircuts, direct and indirect costs, and the effect of recovery delay. Higher recoveries in good conditions can deteriorate alongside PD in stress; treating the two inputs as independent is a substantive assumption. [Schuermann](/references.html#schuermann-2004-lgd) and [Altman and coauthors](/references.html#altman-et-al-2005-recovery) discuss recovery risk.

Under IFRS 9's general approach, Stage 1 measures losses arising from defaults possible in the next twelve months; a significant increase in credit risk moves the allowance to lifetime losses in Stage 2. Stage 3 identifies credit impairment. Twelve-month ECL is not limited to cash shortfalls paid within twelve months. Staging uses forward-looking evidence; past-due backstops do not replace that assessment. [IFRS 9, section 5.5](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/pdf-standards/english/2022/issued/part-a/ifrs-9-financial-instruments.pdf?bypass=on)

CECL under ASC 326-20 measures lifetime expected credit losses without the IFRS Stage-1-to-Stage-2 horizon switch, using historical experience, current conditions and reasonable supportable forecasts, with reversion where needed. The Python calculation below executes the IFRS staging path; its lifetime result is not labelled a complete CECL implementation. [FASB's credit-loss overview](https://fasb.org/page/PageContent?pageId=%2Fstandards%2FTransition%2Fcredit-losses-transition.html) and [forecast guidance](https://fasb.org/page/PageContent?pageId=%2Fprojects%2Fother-staff-projects%2Ffasb-staff-qatopic-326-no-2developing-an-estimate-of-expect.html)

The native simplified ECL integration is inspectable:

$$
\mathrm{ECL}=\sum_i [F(t_i)-F(t_{i-1})],\mathrm{LGD}_i,\mathrm{EAD}(\bar t_i),(1+\mathrm{EIR})^{-\bar t_i}.
$$

Here $F$ is cumulative **physical** PD and $\bar t_i$ is the bucket midpoint. EIR discounting is not WACC or a substituted risk-neutral hazard curve. The exposure's staging PD inputs are lifetime PDs; its measurement schedule has its own cumulative term structure. Keep those horizons aligned.

```python exec id=borrower-covenant-forecast role=build

import math
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
```

### 4. Build a cumulative PD term structure

The three-state example is deliberately small enough to audit. Extract an annual generator, project it to one and five years, and reconcile the five-year projection against five matrix compositions. The emitted JSON is a native calibration artifact. Its regularization mass is zero for this embeddable example; the reconstruction error is near machine precision.

{/* notebook-block notebook="04_statement_modeling/models/ecl_ifrs9_and_cecl.ipynb" cell="rating-generator-term-structure" role="build" */}
```python exec id=rating-generator-term-structure role=build
from pathlib import Path
from finstack_quant.models.credit import migration

rating_scale = migration.RatingScale.custom(["A","B","D"])
annual_transition = migration.TransitionMatrix(rating_scale,[[0.94,0.05,0.01],[0.01,0.94,0.05],[0,0,1]],1.0)
credit_generator = migration.GeneratorMatrix.from_transition_matrix_with_tol(annual_transition,1e-8)
one_year = migration.project(credit_generator,1.0)
five_year = migration.project(credit_generator,5.0)
assert np.allclose(one_year.to_matrix(),annual_transition.to_matrix(),atol=1e-8)
assert np.allclose(five_year.to_matrix(),np.linalg.matrix_power(np.asarray(annual_transition.to_matrix()),5),atol=1e-8)
assert credit_generator.round_trip_error < 1e-8
assert np.allclose(np.asarray(credit_generator.to_matrix()).sum(axis=1),0,atol=1e-12)
pd_term = [(t,migration.project(credit_generator,t).probability("B","D")) for t in [0.5,1,2,3,5]]
assert all(pd_term[i+1][1]>=pd_term[i][1] for i in range(len(pd_term)-1))
assert five_year.probability("B","D") != 5*annual_transition.probability("B","D")
Path("credit-generator.json").write_text(credit_generator.to_json())
print("Annual generator (per year):",credit_generator.to_matrix())
print("Extraction regularization L1 / round-trip error:",credit_generator.regularization_l1,credit_generator.round_trip_error)
```

```python exec id=staged-borrower-ecl role=build
quarterly_times = np.arange(0,5.001,0.25)
physical_schedules = {name:[(float(t),1-(1-credit_data["credit"][name]["pd"])**float(t)) for t in quarterly_times]
                      for name in ["base","downside","severe"]}

staging_policy = StagingConfig(pd_delta_absolute=0.05,pd_delta_relative=float("inf"))
performing_comparator = Exposure("performing-comparator",ead43,lgd43,eir43,maturity43,base_lifetime_pd,base_lifetime_pd)

watched_borrower = Exposure("BORROWER-TL",ead43,lgd43,eir43,maturity43,base_lifetime_pd,base_lifetime_pd,qualitative_flags=QualitativeFlags(watchlist=True))
defaulted_comparator = Exposure("defaulted-comparator",ead43,lgd43,eir43,maturity43,1.0,base_lifetime_pd,dpd=120)
for exposure in [performing_comparator,watched_borrower,defaulted_comparator]:
    stage = classify_stage(exposure,staging_policy)
    print(exposure.id,stage.stage.value,stage.triggers)
assert classify_stage(watched_borrower,staging_policy).stage.value=="stage2"
base_ecl = compute_ecl(performing_comparator,physical_schedules["base"],stage="stage1",bucket_width_years=0.25)
lifetime_ecl = compute_ecl(watched_borrower,physical_schedules["base"],stage="stage2",bucket_width_years=0.25)
manual_stage1 = sum((physical_schedules["base"][i+1][1]-physical_schedules["base"][i][1])*ead43*lgd43/(1+eir43)**((quarterly_times[i]+quarterly_times[i+1])/2) for i in range(4))
assert abs(base_ecl.ecl-manual_stage1)<1e-8
assert lifetime_ecl.ecl > base_ecl.ecl
scenario_weights = {"base":0.60,"downside":0.25,"severe":0.15}
```
