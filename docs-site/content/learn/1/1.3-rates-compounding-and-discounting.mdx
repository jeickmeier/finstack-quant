<DeskContext question={"Is 5% simple, annual, semi-annual, or continuous \u2014 and how much money is the difference on $100mm?"}>

A screen shows 5%, but the treasury desk asks what is actually paid on USD 100m in two years. A simple annualized quote, an annually compounded rate and a continuously compounded rate imply different growth at the same numerical 5%. You must either specify the basis or convert the quote before discounting.

This lesson ends with the first bond PV: actual instrument cashflows multiplied by discount factors and reconciled to the native pricer. It also prices a historical compounded SOFR coupon from its observations.

</DeskContext>

<Standard>

### Rates describe a discount factor

For a payment of one at time $T$, the discount factor $D(T)$ is today's price. With $T$ in the stated year basis,

$$D(T)=\frac{1}{1+r_sT}=(1+r_a)^{-T}=\left(1+\frac{r_m}{m}\right)^{-mT}=e^{-r_cT}.$$

The rates on the right are equivalent only when converted. Holding their numerical value at 5% instead produces different DFs. For two years, simple discounting gives the highest PV in the comparison and continuous compounding the lowest. Conversely, once $D(T)$ is fixed, each representation can be recovered algebraically. These are quoting identities, not competing forecasts of realized investment returns. [Hull](/references.html#hull-options-futures) provides the standard pricing framework.

A continuously compounded zero rate satisfies $z(T)=-\log D(T)/T$. A continuously compounded **interval** forward over $[a,b]$ satisfies

$$f_{a,b}=\frac{\log D(a)-\log D(b)}{b-a},\qquad D(b)=D(a)e^{-f_{a,b}(b-a)}.$$

A simple forward instead uses $(D(a)/D(b)-1)/(b-a)$. The instantaneous forward is $f(t)=-d\log D(t)/dt$, a limit of interval forwards; it is particularly sensitive to interpolation. All these identities must use the curve's time basis. Mixing ACT/360 coupon fractions with ACT/365F curve times without conversion changes the economics.

### Present value and cashflow timing

For deterministic future cashflows in one currency,

$$PV=\sum_{t_i>0}C_iD(t_i).$$

The sign belongs to the holder's perspective. The formula values promised cashflows under the selected discounting treatment; credit loss and embedded options require their own models. Here the USD corporate bond has no hazard curve, so its base price is the discounted contractual stream; its later CS01 uses the spread convention explained in 2.2.

The exported cashflow amount is an undiscounted amount. Multiplying an already discounted `pv` field by a DF again would double discount. Payments already made at the valuation boundary are excluded consistently with the native pricing convention. Keep the valuation date, quote settlement date and payment date separate; clean/dirty settlement reporting is handled in 2.1.

### Overnight compounding

SOFR, €STR, SONIA and TONA are overnight benchmarks with different currencies, calendars and publication rules. A compounded-in-arrears coupon is determined through the accrual period rather than fixed entirely at its start. In its simplest form,

$$R_{\mathrm{period}}=\prod_i(1+r_i d_i/B)-1,\qquad C=N R_{\mathrm{period}}.$$

Here $d_i$ is the number of calendar days for which observation $i$ applies and $B$ is the overnight basis. A Friday fixing commonly spans a weekend, so replacing every weight by one understates interest. Lookbacks, observation shifts, lockouts/cutoffs, payment lags and spread compounding can alter the contractual calculation; the benchmark's currency alone does not specify them.

The native example uses historical synthetic SOFR observations, ACT/360, no reset lag, a weekends-only calendar and compounded-in-arrears treatment. The hand product uses weights 1,1,1,1,3 for a seven-day week. It is checked against the native cashflow builder. See the [ARRC SOFR guide](/references.html#arrc-sofr-users-guide), [€STR methodology](/references.html#ecb-estr-methodology), [SONIA features](/references.html#boe-sonia-key-features), [TONA reference](/references.html#boj-tona) and [ISDA 2021 definitions](/references.html#isda-2021-definitions) for the relevant convention families.

A discount curve prices payment timing. A projection curve supplies future index rates; a fixing series supplies observations already known. These are separate objects even when a simplified single-curve model makes them numerically consistent. Missing a past fixing is not permission to replace it with today's projected forward.

### Four quotes and one native PV

The USD 100m comparison holds the numerical quote fixed. The bond calculation uses actual exported future amounts and the curve day count; agreement within one cent is the reconciliation gate.

{/* notebook-block notebook="01_foundations/market_data/discount_curves.ipynb" cell="discount-proof" role="build" */}
```python exec id=discount-proof role=build
import json, math
from datetime import date
from finstack_quant.core.dates import DayCount
from finstack_quant.valuations.instruments import price_instrument, instrument_cashflows_json
from _shared.analyst_book import build_market, instruments
AS_OF = date(2025, 1, 15)

r, years, notional = 0.05, 2.0, 100_000_000.0
discounts = {'simple': 1 / (1 + r * years), 'annual': (1 + r) ** -years,
             'semiannual': (1 + r / 2) ** (-2 * years), 'continuous': math.exp(-r * years)}
market = build_market('foundations')
curve = market.get_discount('USD-OIS')
bond = json.dumps(instruments('foundations')['USD-CORP'])
flows = json.loads(instrument_cashflows_json(bond, market, AS_OF, 'discounting'))
manual_pv = sum(flow['amount'] * curve.df(DayCount.ACT_365F.year_fraction(AS_OF, date.fromisoformat(flow['date'])))
                for flow in flows['flows'] if date.fromisoformat(flow['date']) > AS_OF)
native_pv = price_instrument(bond, market, AS_OF).price
print({'convention_pvs': {basis: notional * df for basis, df in discounts.items()}, 'manual_bond_pv': manual_pv})
```

### Convert bases at an unchanged discount factor

This second experiment holds economics fixed. The four derived rate numbers differ while their reconstructed discount factors agree.

{/* notebook-block notebook="01_foundations/market_data/discount_curves.ipynb" cell="rate-basis-conversions" role="build" */}
```python exec id=rate-basis-conversions role=build
import math
from _shared.analyst_book import build_market
curve=build_market('foundations').get_discount('USD-OIS')
t=2.0;df=curve.df(t)
equivalent={'simple':(1/df-1)/t,'annual':df**(-1/t)-1,'semiannual':2*(df**(-1/(2*t))-1),'continuous':-math.log(df)/t}
reconstructed=[1/(1+equivalent['simple']*t),(1+equivalent['annual'])**(-t),(1+equivalent['semiannual']/2)**(-2*t),math.exp(-equivalent['continuous']*t)]
print({'DF':df,'equivalent_decimal_rates':equivalent})
```

### Build a compounded-in-arrears coupon

The historical fixing series already belongs to the foundation market. The hand product and native coupon use the same observation calendar and day weights.

{/* notebook-block notebook="01_foundations/market_data/discount_curves.ipynb" cell="overnight-proof" role="build" */}
```python exec id=overnight-proof role=build
import json, math
from datetime import date
from decimal import Decimal
from finstack_quant.cashflows.builder import CashFlowSchedule, FloatingCouponSpec, FloatingRateSpec, OvernightCompoundingMethod, ScheduleParams
from finstack_quant.core.dates import DayCount
from finstack_quant.core.market_data import ForwardCurve, MarketContext
from finstack_quant.core.money import Money

from _shared.analyst_book import build_market
overnight_market = build_market('foundations')
floating_rate = FloatingRateSpec(index_id='USD-SOFR', spread_bp=Decimal('0'), reset_frequency='1W',
    reset_lag_days=0, fixing_calendar_id='weekends_only', overnight_basis=DayCount.ACT_360,
    overnight_compounding=OvernightCompoundingMethod.COMPOUNDED_IN_ARREARS)
schedule = (CashFlowSchedule.builder().principal(Money(1_000_000.0, 'USD'), date(2025, 1, 6), date(2025, 1, 13))
    .floating_cf(FloatingCouponSpec(rate_spec=floating_rate,
        schedule=ScheduleParams(frequency='1W', day_count=DayCount.ACT_360, calendar_id='weekends_only')))
    .build(overnight_market))
overnight_flows = json.loads(schedule.to_json())['flows']
coupon = next(flow for flow in overnight_flows if flow['kind'] == 'float_reset')
period_return = math.prod(1 + 0.05 * days / 360 for days in (1, 1, 1, 1, 3)) - 1
print({'compounded_period_return': period_return, 'coupon': coupon['amount']})
```

<Exercise id="exercise-curve-steepening" title="B. Reprice under a steepener" question={"Apply a zero-rate shock rising to 10bp at 10y, then value USD 1m paid at 5y."}>

At the explicit 5y knot the zero shock is 5bp. The new PV is the original multiplied by exp(−0.0005×5); the long future receipt loses value. An explicit knot removes an interpolation ambiguity in this simple exercise, and the original curve stays unchanged.

{/* notebook-block notebook="01_foundations/market_data/discount_curves.ipynb" cell="exercise-curve-steepening" role="exercise" */}
```python exec id=exercise-curve-steepening role=exercise
from datetime import date
import math
from finstack_quant.core.market_data import DiscountCurve
from _shared.analyst_book import build_market
AS_OF = date(2025, 1, 15)
curve=build_market('foundations').get_discount('USD-OIS');before=curve.df(5)
steep=DiscountCurve('USD-STEEP',AS_OF,[(t,curve.df(t)*math.exp(-0.001*(t/10)*t)) for t in sorted(set(curve.knots)|{5.0})],interp='log_linear')
base_pv,shock_pv=1000000*curve.df(5),1000000*steep.df(5)
print({'cashflow_USD':1000000,'payment_year':5,'zero_shock_bp':5,'base_PV':base_pv,'steepened_PV':shock_pv,'PnL':shock_pv-base_pv})
```

<CheckYourself>

1. **Can the same numerical 5% quote imply different PVs?** Yes; its compounding basis and year fraction must be specified.
2. **What is the curve primitive here?** Discount factors. Zero and forward representations must reconstruct them.
3. **Why does the Friday fixing receive weight three?** It accrues through the weekend in the specified calendar.
4. **What supplies a fixing before the valuation date?** The historical observation series, not a projected forward.
5. **Why distinguish projection and discount curves?** They answer different contractual and valuation questions, even in a single-curve approximation.
