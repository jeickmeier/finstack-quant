<DeskContext question={"The coupon says 5% \u2014 why did we accrue $13,611 and not $13,889?"}>

A coupon rate alone does not determine a coupon amount. You also need the principal, accrual start and end, day-count convention, and any irregular-period rule. A payment holiday can move the cash date without changing the contractual accrual dates. Confusing those two timelines is a common source of differences that look like pricing errors.

You will construct a schedule with an irregular first or final period, explain its coupon fractions, and distinguish the dates used by futures, CDS and listed options.

</DeskContext>

<Standard>

### Four measures of the same period

For a simple coupon, $C=N r\alpha$. The fraction $\alpha$ encodes the contract's time convention. From 15 January to 15 July 2024, there are 182 actual days. On the book's USD 2m bond at 5.5%, changing the convention changes cash, even though the rate is identical.

| Convention | Fraction for that period | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ACT/360 | $182/360$ | Actual days, fixed 360 divisor |
| ACT/365F | $182/365$ | Actual days, fixed 365 divisor |
| 30/360 bond basis | $180/360$ | Adjusted month/day count |
| ACT/ACT ISDA | $182/366$ | This period lies within a leap year |

The start is included and the end excluded. For an ACT/ACT ISDA interval spanning New Year, split actual days by calendar year and apply 365 or 366 to each piece. ACT/ACT ICMA instead relates days to the reference coupon period and payment frequency. For a partial semiannual period it is actual elapsed days divided by twice the reference period's actual days. An irregular coupon may require several quasi-coupon reference periods; dividing by the current calendar year's length is not an ICMA substitute.

The 30/360 family is not one universal algorithm. US/SIA, European, Italian and ISDA variants differ around day 31 and February month end; 30E/360 ISDA also treats termination dates specially. The printed variant comparison makes those boundary rules observable. Use the governing term sheet, [ISDA definitions](/references.html#isda-2006-definitions) or [ICMA rules](/references.html#icma-rule-book) to select the convention; the fact that a library accepts a convention does not make it the right one for a trade.

### Accrual dates, reset dates, payment dates

A holiday calendar determines business days. A business-day convention decides what to do when a contractual date is not one:

- Following moves forward to the next business day.
- Preceding moves backward.
- Modified Following moves forward unless that crosses the month, in which case it moves backward.

31 May 2025 is a Saturday. Following in `usny` gives 2 June; Modified Following gives 30 May. A business-day lag is counted on its specified calendar, not by adding the same number of calendar days. A floating coupon can have an earlier reset/fixing date and a later payment date. The schedule must state whether accrual boundaries themselves move; adjusting only payment dates and adjusting both dates produce different cashflows. The [calendar reference](/references.html#fed-k8-holidays) provides holiday context, while dates are serialized in [ISO 8601](/references.html#iso-8601) form.

### Tenors and stubs

A tenor of six months is calendar arithmetic, not 182 days. End-of-month rules keep an intended month-end sequence aligned when months have different lengths. Build a regular sequence from a chosen anchor, then specify what happens to the remainder. A short front stub puts a smaller irregular period first. A long back stub combines the final regular period with the leftover days. The latter has a larger accrual fraction and coupon at an unchanged rate. Neither is a harmless cosmetic choice.

The native fluent entry is `Schedule.builder(start, end)`. It returns a builder on which frequency, stub and adjustment rules are set. The exercise uses an explicit long back stub rather than hoping a default will match the contract.

### Derivatives dates

Quarterly IMM futures dates are third Wednesdays. Standard quarterly CDS coupon dates are the twentieth of March, June, September and December; the twentieth need not be a business day, and payment adjustment is a further rule. Listed equity-option expiries commonly refer to a third Friday, with venue-specific last-trading and settlement conventions. `next_imm`, `next_cds_date` and `third_friday` solve different problems. A CDS maturity roll, a coupon payment and a trade's step-in date are also distinct concepts, revisited in 2.5.

### Accrual, adjustments and irregular schedules

The first table changes only the time convention. The schedule examples deliberately choose different stub rules; the special-date assertions prevent the common IMM/CDS substitution.

{/* notebook-block notebook="01_foundations/dates/schedule_building.ipynb" cell="date-proof" role="build" */}
```python exec id=date-proof role=build
from datetime import date
from finstack_quant.core.dates import DayCount, Schedule, StubKind, BusinessDayConvention, adjust, next_imm, next_cds_date, third_friday

start, end = date(2024, 1, 15), date(2024, 7, 15)
fractions = {str(basis): basis.year_fraction(start, end) for basis in (DayCount.ACT_360, DayCount.ACT_365F, DayCount.THIRTY_360, DayCount.ACT_ACT)}
assert abs(DayCount.ACT_360.year_fraction(start, end) - 182 / 360) < 1e-12
assert abs(DayCount.ACT_365F.year_fraction(start, end) - 182 / 365) < 1e-12
assert DayCount.THIRTY_360.year_fraction(start, end) == 0.5
assert abs(DayCount.ACT_ACT.year_fraction(start, end) - 182 / 366) < 1e-12
assert adjust(date(2025, 5, 31), BusinessDayConvention.MODIFIED_FOLLOWING, 'usny') == date(2025, 5, 30)
short_front = Schedule.builder(date(2025, 2, 10), date(2026, 1, 15)).frequency('6M').stub_rule(StubKind.SHORT_FRONT).build()
long_back = Schedule.builder(date(2025, 1, 15), date(2026, 2, 10)).frequency('6M').stub_rule(StubKind.LONG_BACK).build()
assert (short_front.dates[1] - short_front.dates[0]).days < 181
assert (long_back.dates[-1] - long_back.dates[-2]).days > 184
assert next_imm(date(2026, 1, 1)) == date(2026, 3, 18)
assert third_friday(3, 2026) == date(2026, 3, 20)
cds_dates = [next_cds_date(date(2026, month, 1)) for month in (1, 4, 7, 10)]
assert cds_dates == [date(2026, month, 20) for month in (3, 6, 9, 12)]
print(fractions, short_front.dates, long_back.dates, cds_dates)
```

### Reference periods and boundary cases

Supply the ICMA reference coupon explicitly. The ACT/ACT cross-year calculation allocates 17 days to 2023 and 14 to 2024. The 30/360 example deliberately crosses February month end.

{/* notebook-block notebook="01_foundations/dates/schedule_building.ipynb" cell="date-reference-periods" role="build" */}
```python exec id=date-reference-periods role=build
from datetime import date
from finstack_quant.core.dates import DayCount,DayCountContext,Thirty360Convention,days_30_360,adjust,BusinessDayConvention
reference_start,reference_end=date(2024,1,15),date(2024,7,15)
partial=date(2024,3,15)
ctx=DayCountContext(frequency='6M',coupon_period=(reference_start,reference_end))
icma=DayCount.ACT_ACT_ISMA.year_fraction(reference_start,partial,ctx)
assert abs(icma-(partial-reference_start).days/(2*(reference_end-reference_start).days))<1e-12
cross_year=DayCount.ACT_ACT.year_fraction(date(2023,12,15),date(2024,1,15))
assert abs(cross_year-(17/365+14/366))<1e-12
variants={str(c):days_30_360(date(2025,2,28),date(2025,3,31),c) for c in (Thirty360Convention.US_SIA,Thirty360Convention.ISDA,Thirty360Convention.EUROPEAN,Thirty360Convention.ITALIAN)}
adjusted={str(c):adjust(date(2025,5,31),c,'usny') for c in (BusinessDayConvention.FOLLOWING,BusinessDayConvention.MODIFIED_FOLLOWING,BusinessDayConvention.PRECEDING)}
assert adjusted[str(BusinessDayConvention.FOLLOWING)]==date(2025,6,2)
print({'ICMA_partial':icma,'ISDA_cross_year':cross_year,'30_360_days':variants,'adjusted_dates':adjusted})
```

<Exercise id="exercise-coupon-day-count" title="A. Explain the coupon difference" question={"Compute the book USD bond coupon on ACT/360 and 30/360 for the same six-month dates."}>

The difference is USD 2m × 5.5% × (182−180)/360 = USD 611.11 before final currency rounding. It is a contractual accrual difference; neither the principal nor the numerical rate changed.

{/* notebook-block notebook="01_foundations/dates/schedule_building.ipynb" cell="exercise-coupon-day-count" role="exercise" */}
```python exec id=exercise-coupon-day-count role=exercise
from datetime import date
from finstack_quant.core.dates import DayCount
from _shared.analyst_book import instruments
spec=instruments('foundations')['USD-CORP']['instrument']['spec']
principal=float(spec['notional']['amount']);rate=float(spec['cashflow_spec']['fixed']['rate'])
a,b=date(2024,1,15),date(2024,7,15)
actual=principal*rate*DayCount.ACT_360.year_fraction(a,b)
bond_basis=principal*rate*DayCount.THIRTY_360.year_fraction(a,b)
assert abs(actual-bond_basis-principal*rate*(182-180)/360)<1e-8
print({'ACT360_coupon':actual,'30360_coupon':bond_basis,'difference_USD':actual-bond_basis})
```

<Exercise id="exercise-long-back-stub" title="B. Explain the final period" question={"Build the specified semiannual long-back schedule and compare its last accrual to a regular period."}>

The final period runs from 15 July 2025 to 10 February 2026. The builder merges the short remainder with the preceding semiannual period. Its 30/360 fraction exceeds 0.5, so the final coupon is larger at an unchanged principal and rate.

{/* notebook-block notebook="01_foundations/dates/schedule_building.ipynb" cell="exercise-long-back-stub" role="exercise" */}
```python exec id=exercise-long-back-stub role=exercise
from datetime import date
from finstack_quant.core.dates import Schedule,StubKind,DayCount
schedule=Schedule.builder(date(2025,1,15),date(2026,2,10)).frequency('6M').stub_rule(StubKind.LONG_BACK).build()
assert schedule.dates==[date(2025,1,15),date(2025,7,15),date(2026,2,10)]
accruals=[DayCount.THIRTY_360.year_fraction(a,b) for a,b in zip(schedule.dates[:-1],schedule.dates[1:])]
assert accruals[-1]>accruals[0]
print({'dates':schedule.dates,'30360_year_fractions':accruals})
```

<CheckYourself>

1. **Which date changes under a payment-only holiday adjustment?** The cash payment date; accrual boundaries stay contractual unless the contract says otherwise.
2. **Why does ACT/ACT ICMA require more context than ACT/365F?** It needs frequency and a reference coupon period, especially for partial or irregular periods.
3. **Is a long stub merely a shifted payment?** No. It changes the accrual interval and potentially the coupon amount.
4. **Are 20 March and the March IMM Wednesday interchangeable?** No. They identify different market schedules.
